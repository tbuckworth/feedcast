The March finding was that controllability decreased with RL training

Jeffrey Wang
: Across many evals, we see that no-CoT capability (reasoning capability in a forward pass, a proxy for worse CoT monitorability) smoothly improves with better pretrained models (lower base model perplexity). We ask the same about CoT controllability, which measures a model’s ability to control its CoT when prompted to add, remove, or manipulate CoT tokens. There, we also see a (weaker) correlation between lower base model perplexity and higher CoT controllability. Next, we focus on architecture. We compare three different architectures at shared base model perplexity levels. For the majority of no-CoT evaluations, perplexity is predictive across architectures. … In an Astra-class model, we find that CoT controllability increases substantially during RL even with no process supervision other than length constraints and final answer grading. CoT controllability increases slightly when we add tool access and tool supervision during RL on a Terra-class model. Our current results indicate that the effect of full tool process supervision is small compared to decreasing model perplexity. Finally, we study how decisions made in one model generation can affect the next via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on model-generated data. To investigate, we selected two Astra-class teachers with ~matched downstream capabilities but differing CoT controllability.
