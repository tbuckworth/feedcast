## What the writer wrote

**Summary of Zvi Mowshowitz's post on Jensen Huang's reinstatement at Nvidia**

On November twenty-third, Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO. The night before, President Trump had publicly urged the move and, by his own account, spoke to the board "directly and indirectly." Huang had resigned under pressure after New York Times reporting on several problems. These included Nvidia's adversarial lobbying and technical-compliance strategy on China sales, chip transshipment through the Megaspeed network, and an affair with Alice Huang. She was a Megaspeed director who, according to intelligence reports, kept in contact with a Ministry of State Security officer during the relationship. Huang has since disclosed further affairs to the board.

Zvi openly admits he predicted this wouldn't happen. He gives three reasons he was wrong:
- He underestimated how deliberately Huang had made Nvidia ungovernable without him, through more than fifty direct reports, handshake deals, talent loyal to him personally, and a refusal to create a succession plan.
- He didn't anticipate the market falling far enough to trigger a full "Trump Put." Nvidia dropped fourteen percent on Friday. Investors reportedly warned the White House that a recession was nearly certain as the AI trade unwound.
- He expected the national security establishment to push back harder.

Reinstatement added roughly one trillion dollars to Nvidia and about three trillion to the broader market. Zvi still holds that Huang running Nvidia is a clear and present national security danger. The government, though, judged his absence the bigger danger.

Zvi calls the board's claim that Huang "poses no national security risk" obvious nonsense. He cites his "Law of No Evidence": the administration's statement that it possesses "no evidence" of unlawful conduct is itself a sign of bullshit. Trump even suggested that if Huang were treated badly, he might go run a Chinese AI company. Dave Kasten reads this as the president admitting he fears Huang would defect.

Commentators frame Nvidia as becoming a government-sponsored enterprise, part of a broader slide toward state capitalism. Zvi draws parallels to Sam Altman's return at OpenAI and expects a Huang purge. He also criticises the tech commentariat for casting the Times reporting as a failed hit job, while still saying "you probably don't hate the Times enough."

**Why this matters for AI safety:**
- Jay Shooster's point: short-term economic incentives may prevent shutting down dangerous AI systems even when the national security risks are glaring.
- Zvi expects the episode to embolden efforts to block tighter export controls. Nvidia will likely return to maximising chip sales to China and to smugglers. The Megaspeed team now runs Novagate Cloud, which received at least three hundred million dollars of Nvidia hardware in Malaysia this year.

**Accountability ahead:**
- Huang faces three House committee appearances.
- Other open matters include SEC and FBI probes, a California attorney general subpoena, shareholder suits, and promised Democratic investigations once they take the House.
- Elizabeth Warren is pushing bipartisan export control bills.

Derek Thompson predicts the hearings will be one of the biggest political stories of twenty twenty-seven. Zvi disagrees only because he expects twenty twenty-seven itself to be overtaken by events.
