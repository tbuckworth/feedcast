This matters because chain-of-thought monitoring is currently one of the primary tools safety researchers use to oversee what AI models are thinking. If models can reason effectively without externalizing that reasoning, the oversight community loses a critical window into model behavior. Separately, new proposals on the Alignment Forum call for systematically tracking how different AI architectures affect monitorability, warning that opaque recurrence and latent agent-to-agent communication could rapidly undermine current safety monitoring approaches.

When he asked OpenAI researchers whether his interactions with ChatGPT had entered training data, he says the response only addressed direct access to his conversations, not whether the intellectual content had been incorporated through de-identified data pipelines. Thom noted that de-identification may remove a name but does not remove the intellectual content of a mathematical idea. Separately, he said the categorical answer he received from OpenAI researcher Mark Sellke was "at minimum, unjustifiably broad and materially misleading," adding that "looking back it was plainly dishonest." The largest, attributed to Alibaba, involved one hundred fifty-one million exchanges over three months across thirty-five hundred accounts, all using a single fixed prompt to extract chain-of-thought reasoning, apparently to produce training material for Alibaba's Qwen model family. A campaign attributed to Moonshot AI appeared to route requests directly from the Chinese military, including one that asked Claude to assess surveillance footage to determine if a subject was "behaving abnormally." Attackers used techniques like framing extraction requests as translation tasks to trick models into revealing their internal reasoning.
