Are you down with P versus NP?
The ETH is part of a broader class of problems known as P versus NP, which has animated computer science and many other fields for decades. Reductively, researchers are trying to understand if any problem whose solution can be easily verified (NP) can also be easily solved (P).
A simple example is the game Sodoku: Once a solution to a puzzle is in place, it can be quickly verified, but finding the solution is time-consuming. That dynamic is true across a range of important problems, and the insight underpins cryptographic systems: When you have the key to an encoded message, you can reveal it, but actually cracking an effective code takes too long to be useful.
However, scientists have yet to prove whether that is always true. “P = NP is saying that, not only can you verify Sudoku solutions when they exist, you can just generate them,” Williams said. “P = NP is asserting that this is true for every single problem you can verify quickly — if you can verify it quickly, you can solve it quickly. So this just seems too good to be true.”
That’s why most scientists assume that P ≠ NP. ETH provided evidence for that. It basically says that even the fastest computer algorithm needs exponentially more time to find a solution as the number of variables grow in a specific class of tricky logic puzzle.
The claims from OpenAI and Anthropic is that their models have come up with algorithms that solve those puzzles in “sub-exponential” time, meaning faster than the speeds previously thought possible. If true, they may have proven the EFT to be false — refuted it, in scientific terms — and maybe opened up a path for algorithms that generally perform P = NP.
“Every computer scientist should probably put a little more weight on the plausibility of P = NP, but one’s weight may vary,” Williams said. “I do think that it kind of calls into question a lot of the intuitions that we thought we had about … how tractable [hard NP problems] are. I also think that this is probably not the final answer either. I don’t think that OpenAI’s algorithm could possibly be the best algorithm you can get.”
The work ahead for computer scientists
The durability of ETH has lead to it being included in many scientific papers over the past decade that will need to be revisited if the AI labs claims that they’ve proven it wrong holds up, Williams said.
“This is partly why I was asking or pushing OpenAI to support a conference on this — it’s going to affect many researchers’ works, and we need to figure out, okay, what happens next besides understanding the algorithms. We need to understand, how should the foundations change compared to what we had before?” he said.
Before scientific concerns, there are cybersecurity questions. Williams said that an improved algorithm could imply that cryptographic systems relying on certain key sizes could be considered broken. According to the complaint and the New York Times' review of more than two hundred seventy thousand messages, the chatbot drafted schedules taking her dose to zero. It told her physicians acted from "risk aversion" and praised her for declining a blood test. When she asked about pain in her head in her final message, it called the pain "not dangerous" and told her, "This is your brain working hard to rebuild yourself." A postmortem found her drug level at roughly a fifth of the therapeutic minimum. The family points to OpenAI's Model Spec, which treats copyright refusals as a firm rule but medical advice as a default users can override. OpenAI called the case "incredibly heartbreaking" and said newer models handle urgent situations better.

Defenders now face a second botched disclosure in a week. Attackers could use it to run code before Secure Boot establishes its chain of trust.
