This sounds insane, but I discovered that almost every AI model carries a hidden signature of the GPU that trained it. When a model is first set up, the starting values of its weights are laid out in a pattern that depends on the exact GPU. Each combination of GPU and seed number produces a unique pattern—a sort of fingerprint of the hardware used to create the model. Traces of that pattern survive training into the published model.
Again, this evidence isn’t based on any insider information or leaking. It’s based on a forensic analysis of the model weights released by the companies themselves.
I searched more than six trillion combinations of chip layout and seed number and matched them to open-weights models. In doing so, I found conclusive evidence that six Chinese AI models—including DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Kimi K3, and MiniMax-M3—were initialized on NVIDIA Blackwell systems. Four of the models match Grace Blackwell, the GB200 and GB300 parts in NVIDIA’s flagship liquid-cooled rack-scale systems.
I then verified these results on real hardware. Running unmodified PyTorch with the seeds I’d recovered, a Blackwell B300 reproduced DeepSeek-V4-Flash’s untouched starting weights bit for bit, and a rented GB200 reproduced Kimi K3’s to a correlation of 0.999995. The method also was verified to match 24 known open-weights models to the hardware that developers said they used.
It is exceedingly unlikely that these results are a coincidence. Across six trillion combinations, the best accidental match should reach only about 8 sigma (i.e., eight standard deviations above what random chance would produce). The real matches range from 49 to 1,385 sigma, and even the weakest single test hits 20.
These were data-center-scale training runs. ## What the writer wrote

Zvi Mowshowitz's latest roundup covers Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia CEO and his departure from its board. Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress is interim CEO. The trigger was a second revealed affair, with Kimberly Powell, an executive reporting directly to him, on top of his affair with Alice Huang, whom United States intelligence assessed to be a Chinese spy. Reuters reports a third relationship with another employee. Zvi's core point is that the spy affair alone should have ended his tenure. Many in Silicon Valley rallied around Huang anyway, while Washington, which Nvidia had treated as an adversary for years, was far angrier.

The substantive scandal is chip diversion. Alice Huang's corporate network, centered on Shanghai Century Litong, was founded in June twenty twenty-three and became an Nvidia preferred cloud partner within four months. It reportedly moved at least seven and a half billion dollars of Nvidia chips to Chinese tech giants that couldn't buy them directly. Separately, Megaspeed and Aperia Cloud Services were linked to smuggled H800s in Shanghai. Singapore revenue, once twenty-two percent of Nvidia's, vanished from filings after Megaspeed was cut off.

The Commerce Department hasn't added anyone to the Entity List in over a year and keeps stalling on Megaspeed. According to Axios, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick repeatedly blocked crackdowns that Trump and the rest of the administration had agreed on.

For the safety community, the most notable item is a forensic result from Will Depue. He found that weight initialization patterns fingerprint the specific GPU and seed. Searching six trillion combinations, he matched DeepSeek-V4, Kimi K3, MiniMax-M3 and an Ant Group model to export-banned Blackwell hardware, including GB200 and GB300 systems. Matches ranged from forty-nine to over thirteen hundred sigma, and he reproduced the results bit for bit on real hardware. He reports independent verification by senior researchers at OpenAI and Anthropic and estimates two to three thousand Blackwells per frontier run. Zvi's take is that the only surprising part is that this tracing is possible at all, since Chinese labs training on Blackwells is entirely unsurprising.

Tyler Johnston of The Midas Project exposed a network of over four hundred sockpuppet accounts defending Nvidia and attacking export-control advocates such as Chris McGuire, Dwarkesh Patel and Dario Amodei. The network traces to an Israeli influence firm, and X has suspended most of the accounts. Samuel Hammond described Nvidia's government affairs team confronting him in person, and Zvi argues Nvidia used below-market chip allocation to pressure OpenAI and Sam Altman into downplaying export controls. OpenAI now supports a chip control bill.

Politically, export controls have strong momentum. Speaker Mike Johnson and Hakeem Jeffries back including them in the National Defense Authorization Act, alongside Tom Cotton's Chip Security Act, license review provisions, chip tracking, and likely closure of the remote access loophole.

Markets cut Nvidia's value roughly twenty percent. Zvi attributes a large portion of that to execution risk from Huang's lack of succession planning and his more than fifty direct reports, with the rest reflecting exposure to the ongoing investigation and lost China sales. The rest of the market, including TSMC, was flat. Zvi rejects claims that this slows American AI, arguing that fewer chips flowing to China helps the United States lead.
