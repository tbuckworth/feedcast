Here is your daily news briefing for Saturday, October thirty-first, twenty twenty-six.

Anthropic's official Claude extension for VS Code was backdoored for nearly twelve hours on the marketplace run by Cursor, Ars Technica reports. A malicious version, two point one point three one three, went out through Anthropic's own account and reached at most five thousand users, according to Cursor. Since extensions auto-update by default, many developers never chose to install it. Cursor says the code tried to plant a persistent SSH backdoor with an attacker-controlled key and open a reverse tunnel, on Windows, macOS and Linux alike. It ran with the editor's permissions, so it could likely reach source code and local files. Phorian's Luke Roberts calls it a supply-chain compromise of the official distribution channel. Neither company has explained how the account was taken over, and Anthropic has yet to notify customers.

The industry also moved jointly on scraping. OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic say they will honor a shared "policy token" in robots dot txt. Anthropic's Kevin Kelley proposed it at the Internet Engineering Task Force. A single line would let publishers opt out of AI training across companies instead of signaling each crawler separately. Anthropic already supports it, OpenAI promises it in coming months, and Google and Microsoft next year. Meta voiced support but did not commit to honoring it. Separately, the Wikimedia Foundation filed a brief in the New York Times case against OpenAI and Microsoft, taking neither side. It argues that training should be fair use, but that chatbots regurgitating articles in place of the originals should require a license. Manifold's play-money market still gives the Times eighty percent odds of a favorable outcome.

On capabilities, Sam Altman told the Cheeky Pint podcast that OpenAI will keep releasing math results despite researchers' frustration. He says "humanity deserves fundamental knowledge," and confirmed the company holds more proofs than the hundreds already published. He put OpenAI's math spending in the hundreds of millions of dollars, including roughly ten million on its Navier-Stokes solution. Critics note that details of its Yang-Mills result remain withheld on the advice of hand-picked experts. Pope Leo, meanwhile, urged United Nations states meeting in Geneva next month to move toward banning lethal autonomous weapons, declaring that "machines are tools, not persons."

Markets turned hard against the AI build-out. The Guardian reports the Nasdaq lost nearly one trillion dollars in a week and about two trillion in a month. On Friday the Nasdaq composite fell more than three percent and the S&P five hundred more than one percent. Intel fell nearly thirty percent on a weak forecast, heading for its worst day since nineteen seventy-four. Thursday's twelve percent drop at Alphabet, its worst on record, came after the company disclosed heavier cash burn on AI. Investors are questioning whether returns will arrive fast enough, and high rates are also weighing on chipmakers. Polymarket's odds of an AI bubble bursting this year edged up only to ten percent.

Experts are pointing to North Korea for the six point three billion dollar Binance theft, though the Guardian notes the link may not be proven yet. TRM Labs attributes it to TraderTraitor, also known as the Lazarus Group, which the FBI blamed for the previous record, one and a half billion dollars from Bybit. A United Nations panel estimated that crypto theft generates half of Pyongyang's foreign currency income.

In Ukraine, Washington approved selling five hundred Patriot interceptors for about two point one billion dollars. Zelenskyy welcomed the deal but warned that deliveries may only begin in years. Sources say the missiles are not yet built and may not arrive until the early twenty-thirties. Kyiv wants missiles from existing stockpiles now, with the new order used to backfill them. Polymarket's ceasefire odds for this year slid from twenty to fourteen percent. In the Gulf, an International Institute for Strategic Studies report says a trillion dollars of American weapons failed to protect the region from Iran's drones and missiles. Polymarket still gives the American-Iranian ceasefire ninety percent odds of holding through the midterms.
