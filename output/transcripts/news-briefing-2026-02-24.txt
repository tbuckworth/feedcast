Here is your daily news briefing for Tuesday, February twenty-fifth.

The primary story today is a significant escalation in the artificial intelligence trade war. Anthropic has formally accused Chinese firms DeepSeek, Moonshot, and MiniMax of conducting "industrial-scale" campaigns to scrape its Claude model. According to Anthropic, these companies used approximately twenty-four thousand fraudulent accounts to generate over sixteen million exchanges. This practice, known as model distillation, allows competitors to train their own systems using the outputs of a superior model at a fraction of the cost. This development is expected to catalyze ongoing debates in Washington regarding stricter export controls on A.I. chips and cloud computing resources.

In a related and highly unusual move, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has summoned Anthropic C.E.O. Dario Amodei to the Pentagon for urgent discussions. The Secretary has reportedly threatened to designate Anthropic as a "supply chain risk" over the military's use of Claude. While the specific nature of the hardware or software insecurity has not been made public, the summons signals a deepening rift between the administration and A.I. labs over how private-sector intelligence is integrated into national defense.

On the technical front, OpenAI has announced it will stop using "S.W.E.-bench Verified" to measure the coding progress of its frontier models. The lab claims the benchmark has become contaminated—meaning the models have already seen the test data during training—and contains flawed tests. OpenAI is recommending a shift to "S.W.E.-bench Pro" to ensure accurate evaluations. Simultaneously, researchers at Guide Labs have open-sourced an eight-billion-parameter model called "Steerling," which utilizes a new architecture designed to make its internal reasoning more interpretable to humans. This addresses a growing concern among safety researchers regarding "opaque reasoning," where models solve complex problems through methods that developers cannot easily audit or understand.

In geopolitics, the domestic security situation in Mexico has reached a crisis point. Following the death of cartel leader "El Mencho," the Mexican government has deployed thousands of soldiers to twelve states to quell a wave of retaliatory violence. Residents and tourists in affected areas report widespread arson and infrastructure destruction. This instability on the southern border coincides with a diplomatic row in Europe, where a French minister has moved to block U.S. envoy Charles Kushner from government access. The friction stems from comments Kushner allegedly made regarding civil unrest in France, further complicating the administration’s trans-Atlantic relations.

Finally, in the markets, the venture capital landscape is seeing a total breakdown of traditional "conflict of interest" norms. Reports indicate that at least a dozen major venture capital firms that previously backed OpenAI have now also invested in its primary competitor, Anthropic. This shift suggests that institutional investors are no longer picking winners, but are instead "indexing" the entire frontier A.I. sector to hedge against the immense volatility and high burn rates of individual labs.

As we look toward tomorrow, the world anticipates the official expiration of the nuclear ultimatum issued to Iran, with U.S. military assets in the Persian Gulf at peak readiness.
