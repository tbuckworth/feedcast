Here is your daily news briefing for Thursday, May eighth.

The landmark Musk versus OpenAI trial has shifted from philosophical debates to damaging formal testimony. In a video deposition, former OpenAI Chief Technology Officer Mira Murati testified under oath that she could not trust CEO Sam Altman’s private assurances. Murati alleged that Altman lied to her regarding safety standards, specifically claiming the legal department had cleared a new model that had not, in fact, been fully reviewed. This follows separate testimony from President Greg Brockman, who detailed the cutthroat negotiations that led to Musk’s departure. Adding to the courtroom drama, former board member Shivon Zilis—the mother of four of Musk’s children—testified that her relationship with Musk began while she was formally advising OpenAI, a revelation the defense is using to complicate Musk's narrative as a disinterested benefactor.

In the hardware and infrastructure sector, SpaceX is making a massive, vertical leap into the semiconductor market. Reporting indicates Elon Musk’s aerospace firm may spend up to one hundred nineteen billion dollars on "Terafab," a vertically integrated chip factory in Texas. This follows a new strategic partnership where Anthropic will utilize SpaceX’s infrastructure to scale its "Claude Code" platform. Simultaneously, the urgency for domestic chip production was underscored by reports that President Trump has pivotally reversed his stance on AI safety testing. Sources suggest the President was "spooked" by the capabilities of the Mythos model and now views state-led red teaming as a national security necessity rather than a regulatory burden.

In the software layer, Google has abruptly shuttered Project Mariner, its experimental agent designed to autonomously navigate the web. The quiet cancellation suggests Google is pivoting away from general-purpose web agents toward more vertical integrations. This shift coincides with a significant update to Google Search, which will now use generative AI to quote Reddit and other forum-based conversations directly in search summaries. While designed to provide "firsthand perspectives," the move has raised concerns among developers regarding the reliability of training data and the potential for "hallucination loops" if AI-generated Reddit posts are re-ingested by the model.

Geopolitically, the prospects for a de-escalation in the Middle East have brightened. Pakistan is mediating a United States proposal to convert the current temporary ceasefire with Iran into a permanent end to hostilities. President Trump stated today he believes the war could be "over quickly," even as Shell reported quarterly profits of nearly seven billion dollars driven by the recent conflict-induced oil price surge. This optimism is not shared in Eastern Europe, where Russia reportedly ignored Ukraine’s unilateral Victory Day ceasefire, striking a kindergarten in a move President Zelensky described as "spurning" the chance for peace.

Finally, the AI safety community is on high alert after Braintrust, a core provider of evaluation tools for AI engineers, confirmed a breach of its Amazon cloud environment. The company has instructed all customers to rotate sensitive API keys. Because Braintrust provides the "operating system" for testing frontier models, this incident represents a critical supply-chain vulnerability for the very labs currently racing to deploy the next generation of autonomous agents.

Looking toward tomorrow, all eyes are on the Canary Islands as the hantavirus-hit cruise ship approaches port for a potential mass quarantine and further medical evacuations.
