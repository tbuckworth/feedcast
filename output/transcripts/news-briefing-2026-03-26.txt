Here is your daily news briefing for Thursday, March twenty-seventh.

The Trump administration has officially named the first four members of the President’s Council of Advisors on Science and Technology, signaling a shift toward corporate-led policy. The panel includes Meta Chief Executive Officer Mark Zuckerberg, Nvidia's Jensen Huang, Oracle’s Larry Ellison, and Google co-founder Sergey Brin. This group of thirteen will weigh in on national AI strategy, though the appointments have already drawn criticism for prioritizing industry titans over traditional research scientists.

In a dual blow to Big Tech’s liability protections, a jury in New Mexico has found Meta and YouTube liable for social media addiction, awarding a plaintiff six million dollars. This follows a separate trial where Meta was ordered to pay three hundred and seventy-five million dollars. The jury found the companies negligent in designing features they knew were addictive to minors. While the financial impact is manageable for these giants, legal analysts suggest these back-to-back defeats could open the floodgates for hundreds of similar pending cases.

The generative AI landscape is experiencing a sharp correction in both personnel and product strategy. Meta is laying off several hundred employees across its recruiting, sales, and Reality Labs divisions as it reallocates capital toward core AI infrastructure. Meanwhile, Disney’s digital strategy is in flux following OpenAI’s decision to sunset its Sora video tool; Disney has reportedly canceled its one-billion-dollar partnership with the firm. Despite the pullback in video, Google is pushing forward in audio with Lyria three Pro, a new model capable of generating full three-minute music tracks, a six-fold increase over its previous limit.

On the legislative front, Senate Democrats are moving to "codify" safety boundaries for AI. A new bill led by Senator Adam Schiff aims to turn Anthropic’s internal "red lines"—specifically those prohibiting the use of AI for autonomous weaponry and mass surveillance—into federal law. This move follows the Pentagon’s recent blacklisting of Anthropic and reflects a growing congressional desire to ensure human oversight in lethal decision-making. Simultaneously, Senator Bernie Sanders and Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez have proposed a moratorium on new data center construction until comprehensive federal AI regulations are passed, citing environmental and grid stability concerns.

Finally, in a significant security warning, Google has moved the deadline for "Q-Day"—the point at which quantum computers could crack current encryption—up to twenty-twenty-nine. This is years earlier than previous industry estimates, and the company is urging the tech sector to immediately transition away from standard R-S-A and Elliptic Curve encryption to avoid a total collapse of digital privacy.

Looking toward tomorrow, keep an eye on the Philippines as it begins emergency procurement of one million barrels of oil to stabilize its energy grid following the latest disruptions in Middle Eastern supply lines.
