Here is your daily news briefing for Saturday, April fifth.

The push for artificial intelligence to take on high-stakes clinical roles has reached a new milestone in Utah. The state has authorized an A-I system to prescribe psychiatric medications without human doctor intervention, making it only the second instance in the United States of delegating such authority to an algorithm. State officials argue the move will address chronic care shortages and lower costs, but medical associations are raising alarms. Physicians warn that the system’s decision-making logic is opaque and that delegating the management of powerful psychiatric drugs to an A-I carries significant patient safety risks.

Within the industry, Anthropic is aggressively moving to consolidate its ecosystem and expand its reach into biotechnology. The company has reportedly acquired the stealth biotech startup Coefficient Bio in a four hundred million dollar stock deal. Simultaneously, Anthropic announced a major policy shift for its Claude models: as of April fourth, subscribers can no longer use their standard subscription limits for third-party tools like the popular agentic harness OpenClaw. This effectively forces power users into more expensive A-P-I tiers. This move toward monetization comes as Anthropic also launched its own Political Action Committee to influence A-I policy ahead of the upcoming midterm elections.

The internal structure of OpenAI is also shifting as the company manages significant executive turnover. Fidji Simo, who recently took over as the head of A-G-I deployment, has announced a medical leave of absence. This follows the departure of Chief Marketing Officer Kate Rouch, also for health reasons, and a reshuffling of Chief Operating Officer Brad Lightcap into a "special projects" role. Despite this turbulence, OpenAI continues to expand its cultural footprint; following its purchase of a major podcast network, the company is reportedly requiring banks involved in its financial dealings to purchase subscriptions for its Grok and ChatGPT services as a condition of doing business.

In Washington, President Trump has unveiled a one point five trillion dollar defense budget that seeks to fundamentally realign federal spending. The proposal includes a ten percent cut to non-defense domestic programs alongside a surprise request for steep cuts to NASA's budget. This fiscal pivot comes at a delicate moment for the space agency. While the Artemis Two crew is currently halfway to the Moon—successfully transmitting "spectacular" images of Earth from the Orion capsule—the proposed cuts could jeopardize the planned twenty-twenty-eight lunar landing and the agency’s long-term reliance on private partners like SpaceX.

Finally, the geopolitical situation surrounding Iran remains volatile following a series of strikes on industrial infrastructure. While the first French-owned commercial ship has successfully transited the Strait of Hormuz since the conflict’s escalation, tensions remain high. Arms experts are currently disputing the United States' account of a deadly strike on a sports hall in Lamerd, suggesting the evidence may not support the administration's claim of an Iranian missile failure. Domestically, Iranians are bracing for further strikes after President Trump threatened to target bridges and power plants unless Tehran agrees to immediate terms.

Looking toward tomorrow, keep an eye on the European energy markets as leaders meet to discuss reviving nuclear power projects to offset the massive gas and fuel spikes caused by the ongoing war.
