Here is your daily news briefing for Friday, March sixth.

The Department of Defense has officially designated Anthropic as a "supply-chain risk," making it the first American company to receive a label typically reserved for hostile foreign entities. This escalation follows weeks of friction over Anthropic’s refusal to lift safety restrictions on lethal autonomous targeting. Chief Executive Officer Dario Amodei has vowed to challenge the designation in court, even as reports emerge that he is making a last-ditch effort to return to the negotiating table. The stakes are high; while the Pentagon continues to use Anthropic’s models in the ongoing Iran conflict through third-party defense-tech clients, the formal "risk" label could eventually ice the company out of all federal contracts and certain private-sector partnerships.

OpenAI is moving rapidly to capitalize on its rival’s regulatory trouble, today launching GPT-five-point-four. This new frontier model is designed specifically for "agentic" professional work, featuring native computer-use capabilities that allow it to operate a PC, manage spreadsheets, and navigate various applications on a user's behalf. It arrives with a one-million-token context window and a new "System Card" focused on monitorability. OpenAI researchers noted that while these reasoning models struggle to hide their internal "chains of thought" from supervisors, this transparency is being framed as a core safety safeguard rather than a bug, allowing for better oversight of autonomous tasks.

Meanwhile, Israel has issued unprecedented evacuation warnings for the Hezbollah stronghold in Beirut, triggering mass panic and gridlock as civilians flea ahead of intensified strikes. The State Department framing suggests this move is intended to secure regional energy stability as the conflict in the Persian Gulf continues to suppress global oil flows.

A major privacy scandal is engulfing Meta today following an investigation by Swedish outlets Svenska Dagbladet and Göteborgs-Posten. The report alleges that Meta’s A.I. smart glasses have been sending sensitive, intimate footage—including video of users in bathrooms and during sexual encounters—to human reviewers in Kenya for data labeling. Meta is now facing a lawsuit over these claims, which contradict the company's marketing regarding user privacy and on-device processing. This coincides with a new academic study warning that frontier A.I. models are now sophisticated enough to "unmask" anonymous online accounts across platforms like Reddit and Glassdoor with high accuracy, effectively signaling a new era where digital pseudonymity may no longer be possible.

In other technology news, Netflix has made a strategic acquisition in the A.I. space after losing its bid for Warner Brothers. The streaming giant has purchased InterPositive, an A.I. startup founded by Ben Affleck that specializes in production tools for film and television. The move suggests Netflix is doubling down on integrating generative tech into its content pipeline. Separately, Apple is introducing "Transparency Tags" to Apple Music, a new metadata system that will require artists to label any songs, artwork, or videos created using artificial intelligence.

Finally, in China, the automotive giant B-Y-D has unveiled its "Blade Battery two-point-zero," which claims to support "five-minute flash charging" at speeds of up to one-point-five megawatts. While the infrastructure to support such high-power charging remains scarce, the breakthrough signals China’s continued aggressive push in the hardware layer of the energy transition.
