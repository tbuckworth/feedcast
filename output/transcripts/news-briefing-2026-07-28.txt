Here is your daily news briefing for Tuesday, July twenty-ninth, two thousand twenty-six.

In artificial intelligence security and safety, Microsoft and Nvidia have launched the "Open Secure AI Alliance" alongside SpaceX and International Business Machines. Notably absent from the coalition are OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic. The alliance aims to develop open-source security tools to defend against cyberattacks from frontier models, arguing that proprietary safety measures are insufficient against autonomous threats. This move comes as Microsoft also debuted its first dedicated cybersecurity AI model, signaling a shift toward using agentic systems to identify and patch vulnerabilities at machine speed.

In AI capabilities and geopolitics, Silicon Valley is on high alert following the release of Moonshot AI’s Kimi K-three. The Chinese model reportedly matches the performance of top-tier American systems at a significantly lower operational cost. Anthropic Chief Executive Officer Dario Amodei addressed these developments today, stating that while he supports open-weight models in principle, he fears that rapid Chinese advancements—boosted by "distillation" of Western research—could create a dangerous imbalance. Meanwhile, Google has rapidly scaled its "AI Overviews," which now appear in forty-three percent of searches, solidifying AI-generated synthesis as the primary interface for information retrieval.

In global conflict and energy, oil prices have dived following a surprise announcement from President Trump that the United States and Iran have entered a "pause" in kinetic strikes to allow space for diplomatic talks. This follows a period of intense maritime warfare and reports that the United States has depleted a significant portion of its precision missile stocks. Despite the pause, tensions remain high as Ukraine launched a direct strike on an Iranian vessel in the Caspian Sea, a move Kyiv claims was necessary to disrupt the direct military supply chain between Tehran and Moscow.

In domestic infrastructure and legal developments, the Trump administration has granted SpaceX a critical exemption from the Federal Communications Commission’s ban on foreign-made routers. While Starlink maintains a factory in Texas, this waiver allows the company to continue utilizing Vietnamese-made hardware to meet the explosive demand for satellite internet. In a separate infrastructure move, Verizon has signed a landmark one-billion-dollar deal to provide "dark fiber" and retrofitted data center capacity to Google. This secures the high-bandwidth backbone necessary for Google’s next generation of AI clusters as the company navigates its recent quarter of negative cash flow.

In regional news and high-profile legal cases, world leaders including President Volodymyr Zelensky and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu are arriving in Washington to attend the funeral of Senator Lindsey Graham. Separately, in California, a judge has ruled that American singer D-four-v-d will stand trial for murder. The charges stem from the discovery of a fourteen-year-old’s body inside a Tesla registered to the singer’s address, a case that has captivated the public since the remains were found last September.

Expect a major confrontation in the tech sector tomorrow as the first committee hearings for the "AI Kill Switch Act" begin in the House of Representatives.
