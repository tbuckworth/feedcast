Here is your daily news briefing for Saturday, November seventh, twenty twenty-six.

The money behind frontier AI is shrinking fast. Bloomberg reports that OpenAI has raised eleven billion dollars so far in a round valuing it at one and a quarter trillion. The round could total about twenty billion when it closes, well short of the thirty billion it sought in September. OpenAI has also cut its proposed valuation from one point four trillion. Bloomberg separately reports that potential buyers in Anthropic's IPO now put the company at one point two to one point three trillion dollars, and some as low as one point one trillion. A month ago investors were expecting two trillion. Anthropic is also reportedly expected to receive a high "junk" rating from credit agencies. Traders still expect the listing: Polymarket gives an Anthropic IPO this year eighty-eight percent, down only slightly on the week.

New spending data from Ramp may explain the repricing. Token spend grew just one and a half percent across August and September, against forty-two percent in July, even as usage kept rising. Ramp's lead economist, Ara Kharazian, blames a price war rather than weak demand. OpenAI halved API prices for GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna, and Anthropic says in a filing that its own cuts reduced revenue growth by about six hundred million dollars. Customers are also routing work to cheaper models and setting budgets. "You can use more AI, but use it for less," Kharazian said. He warned that "the era of hypergrowth may be over," though October spend did pick up by almost nine percent.

The selloff after the midterms spread worldwide on Friday. The Guardian reports Japan's Nikkei fell six percent and South Korea's Kospi more than six and a half. London's FTSE was briefly heading for its worst day since March before closing down one percent, and Germany's Dax lost two percent. Wall Street steadied, with the Nasdaq closing up one percent. That followed a strong jobs report: the economy added one hundred sixty-five thousand jobs in October and unemployment fell to four point one percent. Jefferies economist Mohit Kumar said he does not see this as "the start of the unwind of the AI trade," arguing Democrats will not abandon a growth engine that creates jobs. Even so, with gas a dollar a gallon dearer than a year ago, economists expect another Fed rate hike in December.

AI-enabled crime is moving into the courts and into security incidents. The Justice Department unsealed charges against three men, Jacob Soall, Sharon Hazan and Guy Sheetrit. They are accused of using AI to fake the voices and images of executives and lawyers, stealing more than one hundred twenty-nine million dollars from victims including hospital systems, a bank and a British tech firm. Separately, Ars Technica reports that Chainalysis says attackers using AI agent-assisted techniques stole credentials and live tokens for all of its users. Its customers include the FBI, Immigration and Customs Enforcement, and the Air Force.

Nvidia faces scrutiny in Washington. The chairs of three House committees have asked Jensen Huang to a closed-door briefing on November eighteenth, after the New York Times reported that intelligence officials believe he was targeted by a Chinese spy operation. Nvidia rejects the claims.

On Iran, Trump says American forces carried out "minor" strikes on an air defence system on Qeshm Island on Friday, the first since the midterms. Central Command is also offering up to twenty million dollars for information on Iran's nuclear program. Trump insists talks continue through Qatari mediation. The maritime intelligence firm Windward reports that Iran-linked tankers have loitered over subsea internet cables near Hormuz, possibly mapping them or placing charges. Manifold's odds of war on New Year's Day rebounded to sixty-five percent.

Finally, Paraguay's Santiago Peña, who tried to resign on Thursday, was impeached and placed under house arrest. Lawmakers had refused his resignation, which would have given him the special jurisdiction granted to former presidents. He faces investigation over favouring Ueno Bank, whose collapse froze the accounts of sixty percent of the country's adults.

All eyes now turn to whether Anthropic can price its listing in a market that has quickly lost its appetite.
