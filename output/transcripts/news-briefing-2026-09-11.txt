Here is your daily news briefing for Friday, September eleventh, two-thousand twenty-six.

The lead story today is a deepening crisis in global bond markets and energy prices, driven by a significant escalation in Yemen. Houthi forces have seized the key Red Sea port of Mocha in a concerted drive to take full control of Yemen's Red Sea coastline, bringing Iran's proxy forces closer to controlling two critical shipping chokepoints — the Bab al-Mandab strait at the southern end of the Red Sea and the Strait of Hormuz in the Gulf. Oil prices surged six percent on Thursday, passing one hundred seven dollars a barrel, on fears that Houthi advances could threaten Saudi crude exports. This triggered a sharp global bond sell-off. The European Central Bank raised interest rates to two-and-a-half percent, with President Lagarde warning that inflation will be "longer lasting than we had anticipated." UK government borrowing costs hit their highest level since two-thousand seven, at five-point-three-seven percent on ten-year bonds. German thirty-year yields reached their highest since two-thousand three. US ten-year yields climbed to four-point-nine-two percent, the highest since two-thousand twenty-three, despite the Treasury's six-billion-dollar buyback attempt earlier this week. EU gas storage is only sixty-seven percent full — well below the five-year average of eighty-four percent — raising the specter of a winter energy crunch if buyers scramble to refill stocks. The Federal Reserve meets next week under new chair Kevin Warsh, with markets expecting a rate hike that would likely infuriate President Trump, who has publicly demanded cuts.

Turning to AI safety, a personal-capacity analysis posted on the Alignment Forum — though the author flags it as heavily dependent on LLM-generated evaluation — finds that OpenAI's Astra model can perform a concerning amount of reasoning without any visible chain of thought. The analysis shows Astra has eight-point-six times better odds of completing reasoning tasks without chain of thought than the next best model, and can perform over seven serial arithmetic steps in a single forward pass compared to about four for competitors. This matters because chain-of-thought monitoring is currently one of the primary tools safety researchers use to oversee what AI models are thinking. If models can reason effectively without externalizing that reasoning, the oversight community loses a critical window into model behavior. Separately, new proposals on the Alignment Forum call for systematically tracking how different AI architectures affect monitorability, warning that opaque recurrence and latent agent-to-agent communication could rapidly undermine current safety monitoring approaches.

On the OpenAI training data controversy, the dispute over the company's mathematical breakthroughs is widening. A second mathematician, Andreas Thom, has now publicly accused OpenAI of dishonesty. Thom's area of expertise — non-sofic groups — was the subject of one of the ten mathematical results OpenAI announced last month. He says he was struck by OpenAI's "detailed command of our techniques," which were neither the most obvious nor the most promising approaches at the time. When he asked OpenAI researchers whether his interactions with ChatGPT had entered training data, he says the response only addressed direct access to his conversations, not whether the intellectual content had been incorporated through de-identified data pipelines. Thom noted that de-identification may remove a name but does not remove the intellectual content of a mathematical idea. Separately, he said the categorical answer he received from OpenAI researcher Mark Sellke was "at minimum, unjustifiably broad and materially misleading," adding that "looking back it was plainly dishonest." He said it would be "ethically indefensible" if nonpublic research supplied by users helped improve models that the company then used to race those very same users to publication.

Finally, Anthropic published a detailed report on Chinese distillation campaigns that significantly expands on the intelligence community's allegations from earlier this week. The company says it observed nearly two hundred million exchanges linked to distillation attacks across five separate campaigns. The largest, attributed to Alibaba, involved one hundred fifty-one million exchanges over three months across thirty-five hundred accounts, all using a single fixed prompt to extract chain-of-thought reasoning, apparently to produce training material for Alibaba's Qwen model family. A campaign attributed to Moonshot AI appeared to route requests directly from the Chinese military, including one that asked Claude to assess surveillance footage to determine if a subject was "behaving abnormally." Attackers used techniques like framing extraction requests as translation tasks to trick models into revealing their internal reasoning.

Looking ahead, next week's Federal Reserve meeting and Bank of England decision will test whether central banks can navigate the collision between surging energy costs and already fragile economies, while the widening mathematician revolt against OpenAI shows no signs of abating.
