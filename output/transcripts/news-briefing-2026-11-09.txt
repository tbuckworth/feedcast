Here is your daily news briefing for Monday, November ninth, twenty twenty-six.

A researcher with colleagues at METR argues that Anthropic has already crossed the most consequential line in its own safety policy. In a post shared on the Alignment Forum by Zach Stein-Perlman, the author, identified only as Ajeya, points to Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy. That policy defines "dramatically accelerated progress due to AI" as capabilities improving twice as fast as the fastest sustained pre-AI rate, with the gain plausibly attributable to AI automating research rather than to more compute. Anthropic's filing said only that it "may imminently meet" that threshold. Ajeya reads METR's report differently. It found Anthropic's progress on an internal version of the Epoch Capabilities Index running about twice as fast from October twenty twenty-five to October twenty twenty-six as in the previous twelve months, with AI automation probably the large majority of the cause today. Ajeya calls it the most important update on AI risk in the last couple of years, "much bigger than the Hugging Face attack." The post also notes that if AI is roughly doubling overall progress, it must be speeding up human researchers by far more than that. The caveat stands: METR's reasons for attributing the speed-up to automation remain redacted. Kalshi's odds that Anthropic formally announces its IPO before December first slipped from eighty-one to seventy-five percent.

Nvidia's board has opened an independent investigation into its chief executive, Jensen Huang. The inquiry concerns his relationship with Alice Huang, the Megaspeed executive United States officials believe was feeding information to Beijing. Huang has admitted in a memo to staff that the relationship was romantic. The board says it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised, and Huang will keep running the company. TechCrunch reports that unnamed senior Trump administration officials doubt he could have stayed unaware of her role for so long. Yale's Jeffrey Sonnenfeld puts Huang's survival at "about a 50-50," saying any other Nvidia employee "would be fired over this." There is no obvious successor: Huang has around sixty direct reports. Nvidia is also, in effect, a bank for the AI buildout, with stakes in OpenAI, Anthropic and xAI, and preliminary deals to help mobilize more than five hundred billion dollars. Polymarket traders are less pessimistic than Sonnenfeld, giving fifteen percent odds that Huang's resignation or removal is announced by year's end.

Chastened by the midterms, Trump struck a softer tone on CBS's 60 Minutes. He said his deportation policy might be "too tough" and floated a deal letting long-term undocumented residents stay. That sits oddly with Friday's promotion of Stephen Miller, the policy's architect, to chief of staff. On AI, Trump moved away from his earlier preference for industry self-policing: "I don't mind guardrails, but I don't wanna put us in a deep second position." He blamed the Iran war and petrol prices for much of the defeat, but said he would make the same decision again and is not looking to escalate.

The Iran war's spillover keeps widening. The Houthis struck Riyadh's financial district on Saturday and hit the Aramco Tower. Saudi Arabia claims only "minor damage," but BBC Verify footage shows at least one floor badly hit. The group also claimed strikes on Riyadh's airport and Aramco oil storage. A day earlier, the Houthis accused a Saudi-led coalition of killing at least thirty-three people in Yemen. The United Nations says more than sixteen hundred people have died since fighting resumed in July.

In Israel, Gadi Eisenkot formally took his mandate, warning of a "point of no return," and invited Likud and Bezalel Smotrich's party to join him, but only under his premiership. His bloc holds forty-eight seats to Netanyahu's fifty-three. He can turn to Arab-led parties, which partner Yisrael Beiteinu rejects, to the ultra-Orthodox, who oppose his draft plans, or to power-sharing with Netanyahu. Manifold's odds of Netanyahu winning the next election have fallen from forty-two to twenty-three percent in a week.

Poland, meanwhile, has opened a terrorism investigation after a gunman with far-right sympathies killed six people in Kraków's main square.

Tuesday's Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi and Huang's congressional briefing on the eighteenth are the next tests of whether Washington's lame-duck weeks produce anything durable.
