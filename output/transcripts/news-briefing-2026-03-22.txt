Here is your daily news briefing for Saturday, March twenty-second.

The conflict in the Middle East has entered a dangerous operational phase as Iran and Israel trade strikes near sensitive nuclear infrastructure. Iranian state media reports a strike occurring near an Israeli nuclear facility, a direct retaliation for what Tehran says was a previous targeting of its own nuclear sites. The Director General of the International Atomic Energy Agency has issued an urgent plea for "maximum military restraint," warning that the normalization of targeting nuclear-adjacent zones marks a catastrophic shift in regional engagement rules.

In Washington, the Trump administration’s Department of Government Efficiency, or DOGE, is initiating a radical overhaul of the nation’s nuclear energy sector. The administration is reportedly inviting Silicon Valley tech leaders into the Nuclear Regulatory Commission to "streamline" permitting processes. Government officials have been quoted as saying the commission will "do whatever we tell it to do," signaling a shift toward a "move fast" philosophy intended to meet the soaring power demands of domestic artificial intelligence clusters. This deregulation push coincides with a separate labor shift in the tech sector, where "AI tokens"—shares of future synthetic compute or model revenue—are increasingly being offered to engineers as a fourth pillar of compensation alongside traditional equity and salary.

The transition to agentic AI has moved from lab testing to real-world deployment on mobile hardware. Tests on the new Pixel ten Pro and Galaxy S-twenty-six Ultra show Google’s Gemini now capable of taking direct control of third-party applications to execute multi-step tasks like ordering food or booking rideshares. While testers describe the current interface as "clunky," the ability for a large language model to bypass standard touch-based U-Is and operate as a "synthetic user" marks the first commercial step toward the app-free future predicted by industry leaders earlier this week.

On the domestic security front, a partial government shutdown has prompted President Trump to threaten the deployment of Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents into major American airports. The President has stated that ICE will "do security" to replace T-S-A staff who have gone without pay for weeks. This potential deployment follows a major legal setback for the administration, as a federal judge ruled today that the Pentagon's restrictive rules on press access violate the First Amendment. The ruling strikes down Department of Defense requirements that forced reporters to agree to specific information-gathering limits to maintain their credentials.

Finally, we pause to note the passing of two figures who shaped the modern American landscape. Robert Mueller, the former F-B-I Director who led the investigation into Russian interference in the twenty-sixteen election, has died at the age of eighty-one. This comes as the tech world also marks a milestone: today is the twentieth anniversary of the very first tweet, posted on March twenty-first, two-thousand-six.

Looking toward tomorrow, the focus shifts to the Pacific as Hawaii prepares for its worst flooding in twenty years, with thousands already evacuated as a major storm system moves across Oahu and Maui.
