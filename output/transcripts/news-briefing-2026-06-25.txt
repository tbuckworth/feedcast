Here is your daily news briefing for Wednesday, June twenty-fifth, two thousand twenty-six.

In a milestone for the semiconductor industry, OpenAI has officially unveiled its first custom silicon, an artificial intelligence processor named "Jalapeño." Developed in partnership with Broadcom, Jalapeño is an application-specific integrated circuit designed exclusively for large language model inference at scale. This move signals OpenAI’s intent to vertically integrate its hardware stack to reduce its massive dependency on Nvidia, while simultaneously optimizing the latency and cost of its current and future models.

The geopolitical and corporate friction surrounding AI safety has escalated as Anthropic publicly accused Chinese conglomerate Alibaba of illicitly extracting capabilities from its Claude models. Anthropic alleges that Alibaba used a network of fraudulent accounts to scrape data, presumably to "distil" the intelligence of Claude into its own proprietary models. This accusation comes at a sensitive time, as European leaders, including the C-E-O of A-S-M-L, are increasingly pushing back against Washington’s expanding export controls, warning that the "chip war" is beginning to stifle global innovation without effectively halting Chinese progress.

On the domestic front, President Trump has submitted an eighty-seven-billion-dollar emergency budget request to Congress to cover the surging costs of the recent military engagement with Iran. The request faces a hostile reception on Capitol Hill, coming only one day after lawmakers passed a war powers resolution rebuking the administration’s unilateral military actions. While the United Nations nuclear chief reports that inspectors are finalizing plans to visit Iranian nuclear sites, Tehran’s foreign ministry maintains that such access is a bargaining chip for a final treaty, rather than an immediate concession.

In the first major domestic political test for the AI industry, a high-stakes proxy war in New York’s Democratic primary has ended in a draw. New York State Assemblyman Alex Bores, who became the center of a twenty-seven-million-dollar spending blitz between pro-OpenAI and pro-Anthropic political action committees, narrowly lost his bid for the twelfth Congressional district. The race was widely viewed as a bellwether for how "big tech" interests will influence future regulation, though the narrow defeat suggests that massive AI-funded campaigns may face significant local voter resistance.

Two major health and environmental crises are converging on Western Europe. France has confirmed its first case of Ebola, involving a doctor recently returned from the Democratic Republic of Congo, prompting immediate quarantine protocols. Simultaneously, a record-breaking heatwave has pushed temperatures to historic highs across France, the United Kingdom, and Spain. The extreme heat is forcing a cultural and political shift in France, where the government is now actively subsidizing air conditioning despite long-standing environmental and aesthetic reservations about the technology.

In the private sector, Agility Robotics has announced plans to go public via a two-and-a-half-billion-dollar merger with a special purpose acquisition company. The humanoid robotics pioneer expects the deal to generate over six hundred million dollars in proceeds to accelerate the deployment of its warehouse robots. In the software space, Figma has unveiled major updates at its annual conference, introducing AI-powered motion graphics and "code layers" as it attempts to bridge the gap between design environments and full-stack development.

Markets will be watching the opening of the Tokyo and New York exchanges following a dual earthquake in Venezuela that measured up to seven point five magnitude, causing widespread infrastructure collapses in Caracas.
