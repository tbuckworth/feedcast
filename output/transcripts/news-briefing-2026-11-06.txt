Here is your daily news briefing for Friday, November sixth, twenty twenty-six.

The outside evaluator METR now says AI is already speeding up AI research at Anthropic. In a report shared on the Alignment Forum by Zach Stein-Perlman, METR staff describe a high-access evaluation that ran from August twelfth to October twenty-ninth. Anthropic gave them internal training metrics, benchmark scores, forecasts and staff interviews. METR's central estimate is that Anthropic is improving its models on well-specified tasks at about twice the rate it managed between March twenty twenty-four and October twenty twenty-five. Its uncertainty runs from one point three to three times. METR says most of that acceleration likely comes from Anthropic's own use of AI. It is confident the speed-up did not come from faster scaling of training compute. It found no clear bottlenecks for the next twelve months, though it admits its view of those bottlenecks shifted several times. Staff told METR the gains come mainly from AI assistance rather than fully autonomous work, but there are signs that autonomous contributions will grow. Anthropic redacted some of the findings METR says most shaped its conclusions. One commenter, who also works at METR, called the report a big update towards superintelligence within one to two years. Another said they were sad that so much of the evidence was redacted. Daniel Kokotajlo said it confirms the fear he raised after Anthropic's filing warned it may imminently hit its scaling-policy threshold.

Washington's new politics are already hitting the industry. The Verge reports that forty-three of forty-four local data center moratoria passed on Tuesday. Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa plans to ban all data center approvals until the legislature passes protections. Wes McEnany, who works with the Future of Life Institute, called the results a "total repudiation" of the AI field. He said any member who votes to wipe out state AI laws will be "looking for a job." Meta's Dina Powell McCormick said building will get "harder" and that firms are weighing construction abroad. OpenAI moved quickly, saying it will stop using non-disclosure agreements with public officials and will pay the full cost of its electricity. Polymarket's odds of an Indiana moratorium by the end of next year edged up from sixteen to eighteen percent.

Investors took it badly. The Guardian reports the Nasdaq fell five and a half percent on Thursday, its worst day in nineteen months, erasing more than two trillion dollars and pushing it into correction. Nvidia dropped nine point two percent and SanDisk twenty-two. The selloff spread to Asia on Friday, with the Nikkei down six percent and South Korea's Kospi down more than eight. Saxo's Neil Wilson called it "a complete capitulation-level event," though "totally overdone."

OpenAI also launched GPT-6 Cyber, a model for finding and fixing exploitable vulnerabilities. OpenAI says it is trained to stay within authorized scope.

Infrastructure attacks are widening. American agencies including CISA and the FBI warned of an "ongoing cyber threat" to internet-connected refrigeration. TechCrunch reports hacks on freezers at American military bases and on a French blood bank's cold units, which were stopped before the blood was compromised. Washington has not attributed the attacks, but security researchers broadly blame Iran. Separately, hoax bomb threats disrupted at least thirty-four European flights this week. Some emails were signed by a Russian far-right movement, though investigators have found no evidence of state involvement. Italy's defence minister called it "under-threshold warfare."

On Iran, BBC Verify analysis of satellite images suggests major excavation near the Karaj and Shahroud missile bases, along with repairs at a dozen other sites. Analysts read it as Tehran moving production underground and bracing for new strikes. JD Vance, meanwhile, blamed the war's unpopularity for Republican losses. Manifold's odds of war on New Year's Day fell from ninety-one to sixty-two percent.

In Paraguay, President Santiago Peña resigned after the collapse of Ueno Bank froze the savings of three million customers.

Next week will show whether markets settle, and whether Anthropic's listing survives them.

On the prediction markets, Polymarket's odds of an Anthropic IPO by November thirtieth fell from eighty to fifty percent; Yahoo Finance attributes this to Thursday's tech selloff. Odds of Anthropic reaching a two-trillion-dollar valuation by year's end dropped from sixty-three to thirty-four percent. PYMNTS, citing the Wall Street Journal, says three major IPO investors doubted a one-and-a-half-trillion-dollar valuation.
