Here is your daily news briefing for Wednesday, March twenty-sixth.

In a stunning reversal for the generative video sector, OpenAI has announced it is shutting down Sora, less than two years after the tool's debut sent shockwaves through the media industry. The company is reportedly abandoning a billion-dollar licensing deal with Disney to refocus its resources on business and productivity tools. Analysts suggest the move reflects a lack of sustained consumer interest in AI-only social feeds and the immense compute costs required to maintain high-fidelity video generation at scale.

While OpenAI retreats from video, the race for agentic commerce is accelerating. OpenAI has introduced the Agentic Commerce Protocol into ChatGPT, enabling visual product comparisons and direct merchant integration. Not to be outdone, Google has partnered with Gap Incorporated to allow its Gemini assistant to physically purchase clothing on a user’s behalf across brands like Old Navy and Banana Republic. Simultaneously, Anthropic has upgraded its Claude Code and Cowork tools with a new autonomous feature that allows the AI to take direct control of a user’s computer—opening files, navigating browsers, and running development tools even while the user is away from their desk.

In a landmark shift for the semiconductor industry, Arm has unveiled its first-ever in-house triple-A-G-I central processing unit. After thirty-five years of strictly licensing designs to others, the United Kingdom-based firm is now a direct manufacturer, with Meta confirmed as its first major customer. These chips are specifically optimized for inference and the deployment of AI agents within Meta’s data centers. This move toward vertical integration coincides with a new three-and-a-half-billion-dollar fund from venture capital giant Kleiner Perkins, which signaled a total commitment to AI infrastructure and late-stage growth.

The geopolitical and legal pressure on Big Tech is reaching a new peak. A jury in New Mexico has found Meta liable for three hundred and seventy-five million dollars in damages for misleading users regarding child safety on Instagram and Facebook. This represents the first major courtroom defeat of its kind for the company. On the security front, the Federal Communications Commission has moved forward with a sweeping ban on foreign-made routers, a policy following the administration's "silicon sovereignty" push. The government will now determine which specific manufacturers receive exemptions, a move expected to create a significant bottleneck for domestic internet hardware.

Finally, international tensions continue to escalate as Russia launched nearly nine hundred and fifty drones at Ukraine over a twenty-four-hour period, the largest such attack of the war. In the Middle East, Israel’s Defense Minister announced the military will seize control of a large buffer zone in southern Lebanon, declaring that displaced residents will not be allowed to return until northern Israel is secured.

Looking toward tomorrow, keep an eye on the American court system, where a series of challenges to Elon Musk’s Department of Government Efficiency could potentially freeze the administration's regulatory overhaul following a surge in litigation.
