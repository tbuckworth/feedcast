Here is your daily news briefing for Wednesday, October seventh, twenty twenty-six.

A human accomplice has surfaced in the Canadian school shooting that became infamous for its links to ChatGPT. The United States Justice Department says it has detained James Codey Bryant, thirty, of western Washington state, on a charge of conspiracy to murder persons in a foreign country. Authorities allege he offered money and "technical advice" to the suspected shooter, Jesse Van Rootselaar, for six months before February's attack, which killed eight people, including six children. According to the indictment reported by CBC, Bryant urged her to delete their messages and suggested a VPN for "posting a shooting video online." Police say he talked her through starting the car after she killed her mother and brother, which let her reach the school, and that he agreed to livestream the attack. They also say he tried several times to dissuade her but never contacted police. Until now it was publicly known only that she had planned with ChatGPT. OpenAI's safety team flagged her internally but did not alert police, and victims' families and British Columbia are suing the company and Sam Altman. A co-conspirator may complicate that case, though it does not erase the question of what OpenAI knew.

OpenAI is also bowing to European law. Ars Technica reports the company will watermark ChatGPT text by default in the European Union to comply with the AI Act, while offering the feature elsewhere switched off. Its proprietary method, called textGrain, hides patterns in word choice that a keyed detector can find, and detector access will go to a limited set of researchers and organizations. Ars notes that existing watermarking schemes are relatively easy to circumvent, and OpenAI's is likely no different.

On capabilities, OpenAI has released seven hundred twenty-two manuscripts from an unreleased frontier model. According to the independent Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence, they contain solutions to "hundreds" of open problems. OpenAI says the average result took the equivalent of three hours of ChatGPT Pro thinking. The papers are on GitHub rather than in established academic channels, which the advisory group had urged labs to use where possible. The group has also warned labs against treating mathematical results as marketing, and The Verge describes a field still struggling to digest the pace of results and to agree on how human mathematicians get credit. In Europe, Mistral released Mistral Large Four, a one-trillion-parameter multimodal model trained on four thousand Nvidia GPUs. For now it sits behind a guardrailed endpoint, and Mistral plans to publish the weights in three weeks once safety testing is complete.

The Black Sea war has spilled into NATO waters. Drones sank two cargo ships and damaged a third inside the exclusive economic zones of Bulgaria and Romania, the first such strikes there, and at least two sailors were killed. Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev called the attack on his country's zone "a blatant violation" of international law. Ursula von der Leyen called the strikes unacceptable but said the circumstances are still being established. Volodymyr Zelenskyy was more direct, saying a joint investigation with Bulgaria's navy found a Russian attack using both sea and aerial drones.

Ebola has crossed into a new country. Kenya reported its first death from the virus, a man who fell ill in the Democratic Republic of Congo and travelled overland through Kampala before flying to Nairobi. Health officials are tracing twenty-three fellow passengers and four crew. The outbreak involves the Bundibugyo strain, which has no vaccine or specific treatment, and it has killed more than four thousand people in Congo. Médecins Sans Frontières warns of an "alarming surge" in North Kivu province.

On Wall Street, the S&P five hundred topped seven thousand eight hundred for the first time, as chipmaker gains and easing Treasury yields lifted both it and the Nasdaq to records.

The next test of Europe's resolve comes this week, as EU trade negotiators head to Beijing seeking curbs on cheap Chinese hybrid cars.
