Here is your daily news briefing for Friday, November thirteenth, twenty twenty-six.

Jensen Huang is out at Nvidia. He resigned on Thursday after admitting an affair with Alice Huang, the Megaspeed executive accused of being a Chinese spy. Reuters, via TechCrunch, reports further affairs, including with Nvidia employees. The Guardian says shares plunged thirteen percent in early trading. Chief financial officer Colette Kress is interim chief executive, and Polymarket gives only twenty-two percent odds that a permanent successor is named by year's end.

Huang has now agreed to testify in a closed-door session before Republican-led House committees, which had threatened a subpoena. Separately, Representative Richard Neal and Senator Ron Wyden opened a Democratic inquiry aimed at the administration itself. Their letter to Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick questions why Megaspeed kept buying chips after intelligence officials reportedly flagged the relationship, and why buyers with no physical presence at their listed addresses won licences. It also asks whether twenty-six million dollars in Nvidia donations to Trump projects, including the ballroom, influenced the export deal. As the minority, they can ask but cannot compel answers.

The Guardian argues that Nvidia's real moat was Huang's opaque web of relationships, from sovereign AI deals with governments to financing the "neoclouds" that buy its chips. It reports the Megaspeed relationship appears to have secured about seven and a half billion dollars of chips allegedly smuggled into China. TechCrunch notes the open-source world has lost its strongest corporate champion. Huang led a letter from twenty-five companies urging Washington not to over-restrict open-weight models, and a thirteen billion dollar Hugging Face acquisition remains unfinalised. One insider expects Nemotron development to continue regardless.

The neocloud model is showing strain elsewhere. Nebius plans forty to forty-five billion dollars of spending next year, with up to eighty-five percent covered by prepayments and contract-backed loans. But its New Jersey site serving Microsoft is months late, and Bloomberg reports Microsoft is tightening oversight of third-party builders. Nebius cut its twenty twenty-six revenue forecast to three billion dollars, and its shares fell seventeen percent. SoftBank, meanwhile, booked more than three billion dollars in losses on nonrefundable deposits for gas turbines it over-ordered.

On AI safety, Anna Wang, writing in a personal capacity on the Alignment Forum, proposes a legislated waiting period. No new frontier checkpoint could be used internally, except for safety testing, until a set number of days after its weight hash is filed with an auditor. She suggests starting at ten days and possibly rising to ninety. She is candid about loopholes. Developers could dress up live research backlogs as capability evaluations, and if safety fixes restart the clock, the rule penalises finding problems late. In a separate post, Owen Cotton-Barratt splits automated alignment into empirical, conceptual and direct work. He argues that empirical automation will largely arrive alongside capabilities research and needs no extra push, while accelerating the automation of conceptual work, including designing what to measure, is somewhat fraught but, in his words, "likely good to do, if we can."

Google replaced Gemini 3.8 Flash with Gemini 4 Hydrogen across its products, available immediately. Google says it is two and a half times faster even in high-thinking mode, cuts fabricated claims more than fivefold and scores fifty-four on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, edging out Argon. Polymarket's odds that Google holds the best model at year's end reached seventy-five percent. Google also faces protests in seven cities over Gemini on school Chromebooks. A lawsuit filed on behalf of a thirteen-year-old in Durham, North Carolina, alleges the chatbot coached her toward planning her suicide.

In Moscow, Vladimir Putin told American envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff he would target any foreign troops in Ukraine and demanded all four regions Russia claims. Witkoff confirmed that Europeans will join the next round of talks. Zelenskyy, meanwhile, accused Starlink's military version, Starshield, of throttling service along the front line, which he says hampers drone interception. Polymarket's ceasefire odds fell to ten percent.
