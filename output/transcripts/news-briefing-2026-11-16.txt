Here is your daily news briefing for Monday, November sixteenth, twenty twenty-six.

We start with AI capabilities. OpenAI has published work on a candidate nonperturbative lattice definition of the Standard Model of particle physics. The company says GPT-6 Astra helped turn the idea into a detailed proof. OpenAI's own summary describes it as "a step toward" a definition rather than a finished result. It is the latest case of frontier models doing work in theoretical physics, and OpenAI has also launched a dedicated science blog to publish more of it.

Now Nvidia, where the fight has moved from whether Jensen Huang should go to whether he can come back. The Guardian reports that more than one hundred sixty-five thousand people have signed an open letter urging the board to "bring Jensen back while an independent investigation establishes the facts." The signatories include Elon Musk, Tim Sweeney, Garry Tan and Travis Kalanick, along with more than seven thousand five hundred people identifying as Nvidia employees, about eighteen percent of the workforce. The Financial Times reports that Huang has discussed a return, and that one ally is assembling a "war room" of Silicon Valley executives to lobby the White House. The BBC understands a reinstatement is very unlikely while internal investigations continue. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told Fox News that Nvidia needs "a dramatic shake-up" and should hire from outside. He said the more important question is whether Nvidia knowingly let its most advanced chips reach China, and he wants answers within ten days.

Megaspeed, the Singapore company at the centre of the affair, broke its silence on Sunday. It called the reports "not supported by verifiable evidence" and said it has hired outside counsel for an internal investigation. It says Alice Huang left her executive role in August last year. The security analysis group C4ADS, however, says she remained "practically in control" of the firm. Representative John Moolenaar confirmed that Jensen Huang will testify behind closed doors to three House committees, with dates still being set. The Verge reports bipartisan momentum for tighter chip export rules, including renewed interest in the stalled Remote Access Security Act. Senate Banking Chairman Tim Scott wants both Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to testify in public.

AI misuse has also become a national issue in South Korea. The top scorer on the national tax accountant exam, who identified himself by the surname Han, confessed to photographing questions with a microcamera, sending them to an AI tool and reading the answers on a smartphone. In the first round he had missed just one of one hundred sixty questions. Seoul police have opened a case. With the college entrance exam on Thursday, the presidential office convened an emergency meeting on countermeasures.

In the Gulf, the lull is over. Iran's Revolutionary Guards launched six drones at a United States radar at a helicopter base in Bahrain, their second attack in three days. Bahrain says all six were intercepted. The Guards also claim a mine sank a supertanker carrying two million barrels of oil in the Strait of Hormuz, which has not been independently verified. The Guardian notes that Trump now says Washington is no longer seeking a deal. Further south, Saudi-backed Yemeni forces withdrew from the Bab al-Mandab area to defend Aden, leaving the Houthis in control of the Red Sea's southern gateway. Saudi Arabia had been relying on that route for oil exports while Hormuz is effectively closed.

On Russia, Estonia will close all three border crossings from December first after what its security service calls a GRU-ordered attempt to bomb and burn the drone maker Threod Systems. Eight people were detained. Meanwhile, Zelenskyy says Starshield speed limits of about one hundred miles per hour persist along the front line and in occupied areas. He says that leaves Ukrainian interceptor drones unable to catch Russian jet-powered Shaheds.

Finally, a United Launch Alliance Vulcan rocket failed on Sunday, destroying forty Amazon Leo satellites in the company's first total mission failure since two thousand six. The rocket is likely grounded indefinitely, which delays Amazon's challenge to Starlink and affects national security launches.

The week ahead brings Bessent's ten-day deadline for Nvidia, South Korea's exam test, and the Shenzhen summit.
