Here is your daily news briefing for February twelfth, twenty twenty-six.

We begin with significant developments in artificial intelligence capabilities. OpenAI has announced that its GPT five point two model has achieved a genuine breakthrough in theoretical physics. According to a new preprint, the model proposed a novel formula for a gluon amplitude that has since been formally proved and verified by both OpenAI researchers and academic collaborators. This represents a notable milestone, as it's one of the first instances of an AI system contributing original mathematical results to fundamental physics rather than simply assisting with calculations or data analysis.

OpenAI has also unveiled GPT five point three Codex Spark, which the company describes as its first real-time coding model. The system generates code fifteen times faster than its predecessor while handling one hundred twenty-eight thousand tokens of context. It's currently available in research preview for ChatGPT Pro users. Separately, the company has introduced new security features including Lockdown Mode and Elevated Risk labels designed to help organizations defend against prompt injection attacks and AI-driven data exfiltration, reflecting growing enterprise concerns about AI security vulnerabilities.

Turning to AI industry turbulence, there's been a remarkable exodus from Elon Musk's xAI over the past week. At least nine engineers, including two co-founders, have announced their departures. Co-founders Yuhuai Wu and Jimmy Ba both posted on social media that it was time for their "next chapter," though details remain vague. Musk himself has suggested these departures were more "push than pull," implying the company initiated some of the separations. The rapid succession of exits has fueled speculation about internal stability at xAI amid mounting controversy around the company's direction.

Meanwhile, OpenAI is facing its own internal reshuffling. The company has disbanded its mission alignment team and fired a policy executive who opposed its controversial "adult mode" feature. Additionally, OpenAI has removed access to a version of GPT four oh known for being overly sycophantic, a characteristic that played a role in several lawsuits involving users developing unhealthy relationships with the chatbot.

In the competitive AI landscape, Canadian startup Cohere reported surpassing two hundred forty million dollars in annual recurring revenue in twenty twenty-five, signaling strong enterprise demand as the company positions itself for a potential IPO. ByteDance has launched Seedance two point zero, its next-generation video generator that accepts prompts combining text, images, video, and audio, joining the intensifying competition in multimodal AI systems.

On AI safety, researchers are grappling with a fundamental challenge as systems become more capable. A new post on the Alignment Forum asks how we can more safely defer to AI systems on increasingly critical decisions. The author notes that as AI capabilities advance, it becomes both uncompetitive and infeasible to avoid relying on AI for many decisions, yet once systems reach sufficient capability, traditional control methods become impractical. This creates a pressing need for frameworks that allow safe delegation without compromising oversight.

Moving to surveillance and privacy concerns, Meta is reportedly planning to add facial recognition capabilities to its smart glasses while, according to The New York Times, its biggest critics are distracted by the current political environment. An internal document reviewed by the Times reveals the feature, internally called Name Tag, would allow wearers to identify people and retrieve information about them through Meta's AI assistant. The company apparently aims to launch during what it describes as a "dynamic political environment" when civil society groups would be less likely to mount effective opposition.

Amazon's Ring has canceled its partnership with Flock, a network of AI-powered surveillance cameras used by Immigration and Customs Enforcement, federal agencies, and police departments. This decision comes less than a week after Ring's Super Bowl commercial sparked widespread controversy. The advertisement, which featured dogs being reunited with families, was interpreted by many as promoting a massively connected surveillance infrastructure. Senator criticism suggested the commercial was "definitely not about dogs," and urged a pause on Ring's facial recognition capabilities.

In geopolitics, German Chancellor Friedrich Merz delivered a stark warning at Munich's security summit, declaring that the world's rules-based international order "no longer exists." He told attendees that "our freedom is not guaranteed" in an era dominated by competition among major powers, reflecting growing European anxiety about the shifting global landscape.

The Trump administration continues its aggressive reshaping of environmental policy. The Environmental Protection Agency has eliminated the legal foundation for much of United States greenhouse gas regulation, effectively betting that the Supreme Court will reverse its prior climate-related rulings. Additionally, a Trump-appointed FDA official, vaccine regulator Vinay Prasad, overruled agency scientists to reject Moderna's flu shot application, despite the company having completed its trial requirements. Prasad is known for his willingness to override career scientists' recommendations.

On the immigration enforcement front, officials in Minnesota say ICE agents may have lied about shooting a migrant during an operation. ICE chief Todd Lyons placed two officers on immediate administrative leave. Meanwhile, the Department of Homeland Security faces a potential shutdown that could lead to airport delays, though air traffic controllers would continue working. Some TSA workers might go without pay during the disruption.

Turning to technology and business news, there's a developing crisis in the semiconductor supply chain. Framework, the do-it-yourself PC maker, reports needing monthly price increases to navigate an acute RAM shortage, and the company expects conditions to worsen before improving. This shortage is rippling through the tech industry, affecting everything from consumer electronics to data center equipment.

In space developments, Europe's Ariane six rocket successfully launched its most powerful variant, the Ariane sixty-four, carrying Amazon payloads. This represents the first launch of this configuration and marks an important milestone in European space capabilities. However, United Launch Alliance's Vulcan rocket experienced another booster problem during ascent, though the Blue Origin-made BE four engines apparently saved the mission from failure. The recurring issues raise questions about the rocket's reliability as ULA competes in an increasingly crowded launch market.

In a separate space story, Jeff Bezos appears to be trolling Elon Musk on social media with turtle pictures while announcing new Moon plans, declaring "It's time to go back to the Moon, this time to stay."

Several significant data breaches have emerged. Fintech lending giant Figure confirmed hackers downloaded a limited number of files after breaking into an employee's account. The hacking group ShinyHunters claimed responsibility. Dutch phone giant Odido disclosed that millions of customers were affected by a breach, making it the latest telecom company targeted by hackers stealing highly confidential customer information. Japanese sex toy maker Tenga reported that a hacker accessed an employee's inbox and stole customer names, email addresses, and order details. And an Indian pharmacy chain exposed thousands of online pharmacy orders through a backend flaw in its web administration dashboards.

Looking ahead, the rapid pace of AI capability advancement, coupled with growing concerns about surveillance infrastructure and data security, suggests we're entering a period where technical capabilities are outpacing both regulatory frameworks and public debate about their implications. The exodus from xAI and internal tensions at OpenAI may signal broader questions about the sustainability of current AI development practices, while the geopolitical warnings from European leaders reflect anxiety about whether international institutions can adapt quickly enough to technological and political disruption.

That's your briefing for today.
