Here is your daily news briefing for Tuesday, March twenty-fifth.

In a significant milestone for the industry, Nvidia Chief Executive Officer Jensen Huang stated on the Lex Fridman podcast that he believes Artificial General Intelligence, or A-G-I, has now been achieved. While the term remains subject to debate, Huang’s declaration marks the first time the head of the world’s most valuable chipmaker has signaled that AI capabilities have met this long-sought threshold. Amidst this claim, Huang also defended the company’s new D-L-S-S five technology against "AI-slop" criticisms, arguing that the neural rendering is a tool for developers who remain free to opt-out if the aesthetic does not meet their standards.

The geopolitical tension between the Pentagon and the AI sector is intensifying. Senator Elizabeth Warren has formally accused the Department of Defense of "retaliatory" behavior following its decision to label Anthropic a national security risk. In a letter to Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, Warren suggested the "unacceptable risk" designation was an overreach, noting the Pentagon could have simply terminated its contracts rather than blacklisting the firm. This friction comes as the F-B-I issued a new warning that Iranian state-sponsored hackers are leveraging Telegram to deploy malware against dissidents and journalists as the kinetic conflict continues.

There are signs of a potential diplomatic breakthrough in the Middle East. Global oil prices fell and stock markets rebounded today after President Trump announced that "very good and productive" talks have been held to end the war with Iran. The shift toward diplomacy follows a period of extreme economic strain, which today saw Slovenia become the first European Union nation to implement fuel rationing, limiting motorists to fifty liters per day. Domestically, DoorDash has launched a relief fund for drivers to offset soaring fuel costs, while China has also moved to cap fuel price hikes to ease the burden on its internal logistics.

On the domestic security front, the Department of Homeland Security is undergoing a rapid transition under new leadership. The Senate has confirmed Markwayne Mullin as the new Secretary of Homeland Security, tasking him with stabilizing a department currently managing multiple immigration enforcement controversies. Simultaneously, the deployment of over one hundred Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents to fourteen major United States airports has begun. These agents are tasked with managing security queues to compensate for T-S-A staffing shortages caused by the ongoing partial government shutdown.

In hardware and cybersecurity news, a major exploit kit known as "DarkSword" has been leaked publicly on GitHub. Cybersecurity researchers warn the kit can be used to target millions of iPhones running older versions of i-O-S with sophisticated spyware. This security lapse coincides with a new federal ban on the import of foreign-made consumer internet routers. Given that almost no major router brands are currently manufactured within the United States, the move is expected to cause significant disruption to the domestic hardware market as the administration pushes for "silicon sovereignty."

Finally, we note the passing of Leonid Radvinsky, the billionaire owner of OnlyFans, who has died at the age of forty-three following a battle with cancer.

Looking toward tomorrow, the tech world turns its attention to a courtroom in Iowa, where a group of parents is set to sue a local school district following the sentencing of several teenagers who used AI to generate non-consensual explicit imagery of their classmates.
