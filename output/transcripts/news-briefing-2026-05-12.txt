Here is your daily news briefing for Tuesday, May thirteenth.

The cyber security landscape has shifted toward automated warfare as both defensive and offensive AI capabilities reach critical milestones. OpenAI has announced "Daybreak," a new initiative built on its March-release Codex Security agent. Daybreak is designed to identify and patch vulnerabilities before they can be exploited by human or machine actors. This launch coincides with a landmark report from the Google Threat Intelligence Group, which confirmed it has intercepted the first-known zero-day exploit fully developed by an artificial intelligence. The failed "mass exploitation" event was orchestrated by prominent cybercrime actors targeting two-factor authentication systems. These developments signal that the long-predicted era of AI-generated malware is officially underway, forcing a pivot toward AI-native defensive telemetry across the global tech stack.

In the artificial intelligence sector, the race for fluid, multimodal interaction is intensifying. Thinking Machines, the new venture from former OpenAI Chief Technology Officer Mira Murati, has unveiled its core focus: "interaction models." Unlike current turn-based architectures where users and models trade responses, Murati’s firm is developing systems that process audio and video inputs in real-time, allowing for a continuous, low-latency collaboration that mimics human conversation. Simultaneously, OpenAI is moving to entrench its ecosystem within the next generation of developers by launching the OpenAI Campus Network. This global initiative will provide student clubs with direct access to frontier models and specialized training tools, effectively creating a dedicated pipeline of AI-literate talent.

The legal and corporate fallout from the rapid move toward AI integration is accelerating. In court today, the high-stakes battle between Elon Musk and Sam Altman continued to expose the friction between OpenAI’s non-profit roots and its current commercial scale. This corporate restructuring is having immediate effects on the workforce; General Motors has laid off hundreds of Information Technology workers, explicitly stating that the move is intended to clear headcount for new hires specializing in AI-native development and agentic workflows. Even legacy media is adapting, as Digg announced it will relaunch as an AI-driven news aggregator to compete with an increasingly fragmented digital landscape.

Geopolitically, the month-long ceasefire in the Middle East is on "massive life support." President Trump today characterized the current diplomatic status as "unbelievably weak" following an Iranian counteroffer that he dismissed as a non-starter. This instability continues to drive energy market anxiety, with JP Morgan analysts now predicting that oil will remain above one hundred dollars per barrel for the remainder of the year, even if the Strait of Hormuz is reopened by next month. The heightened tensions add significant weight to the upcoming summit in China, where President Trump will be joined by seventeen top executives, including Elon Musk and Tim Cook, to negotiate a fragile trade truce.

Finally, on the technology and policy front, a long-standing barrier in digital communication has fallen. Apple and Google have officially rolled out end-to-end encrypted Rich Communication Services, or R-C-S, enabling secure, encrypted messaging between Android and iPhone users for the first time. On the hardware side, the Federal Communications Commission has extended a crucial waiver allowing foreign-made routers and drones to receive security updates until twenty-twenty-nine, a move intended to prevent a massive wave of hardware obsolescence while the United States government pushes for domestic manufacturing alternatives.

Looking toward tomorrow, the focus will be on the initial meetings of the United States trade delegation in Beijing as the "Terafab" initiative faces its first real test of diplomatic feasibility.
