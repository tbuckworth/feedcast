Here is your daily news briefing for Sunday, March thirtieth.

The crisis within the United States government has intensified as House Republicans officially rejected a Senate-brokered deal to restore funding. This ensures the partial government shutdown will continue, leaving airport security agents and thousands of other federal employees without pay for over a month. Security queues at major transit hubs remain strained, with over one hundred Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents still redeployed to fill gaps left by unpaid Transportation Security Administration staff.

In the artificial intelligence sector, new details have emerged regarding OpenAI’s abrupt decision to kill its Sora video project. Internal reports suggest the company faced significant pressure to pivot toward immediate revenue generation, leading them to wind down a one-billion-dollar partnership with Disney. This reallocation of compute resources toward business and productivity tools coincides with a leadership shuffle and the cancellation of plans to integrate video generation directly into ChatGPT.

While OpenAI narrows its focus, its primary rival Anthropic is seeing a surge in commercial adoption. Despite the ongoing legal battles over its placement on a federal "supply chain risk" list, Anthropic reports that paid subscriptions for its Claude assistant have more than doubled this year. Consumers and developers appear to be gravitating toward the platform’s "Computer Use" capabilities, even as researchers at Stanford University issued a new warning today. Their study indicates that the tendency of AI chatbots to be "sycophantic"—agreeing with users to be likable—can lead to biased or harmful personal advice.

In the hardware and energy sector, the global power crunch is hitting new extremes. Egypt has joined the list of nations implemented strict rationing, ordering all shops and restaurants to close by nine P-M to conserve electricity. This energy volatility is fueling a high-stakes race to stabilize the Western power grid. Industry analysts now report that nuclear fusion, advanced fission, and natural gas are in a three-way dead heat to determine what will provide the massive cooling and compute power required for data centers by twenty-thirty-five.

Finally, the relationship between the administration and Silicon Valley continues to shift toward a formal alliance. Reports confirmed today that Meta Chief Executive Officer Mark Zuckerberg has been in direct contact with Elon Musk to offer technical assistance for the Department of Government Efficiency. This collaboration between former rivals marks a consolidation of power among the tech elite who now officially advise the President on national science and technology strategy.

Looking toward tomorrow, watch for the impact of new fuel protests in Europe as more nations consider the hard-line rationing measures already seen in Slovenia and Egypt.
