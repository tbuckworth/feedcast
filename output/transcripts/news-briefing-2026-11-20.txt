Here is your daily news briefing for Friday, November twentieth, twenty twenty-six.

Anthropic is now a public company, and its chief executive used the opening bell to make a promise. Before ringing it, Dario Amodei told the Nasdaq floor, according to TechCrunch, that Anthropic would not bend to business or political pressure. "We will pace the training and deployment of AI systems," he said, "taking as long as is necessary to have confidence that they are safe and will remain under human control." He warned that "the most difficult safety work lies ahead," and said more of the company's talent must go to double and triple checking its systems. Co-founder Daniela Amodei told CNBC the company is "deeply committed to safety."

The market was less steady than the message. The Guardian reports that shares opened thirty-four percent above the eighty-three dollar offer price, at one hundred eleven dollars fifty, briefly valuing Anthropic above two trillion dollars. They closed just over ninety-seven dollars, a market capitalisation of nearly one point three trillion. Most chipmakers fell on the day. The prospectus shows revenue of sixteen point two billion dollars in the first half of this year and losses of eighty billion over the same period, largely due to a one-off charge, growing to one hundred four billion over nine months. On Manifold, the odds that Anthropic ends up worth more than OpenAI after both list rose from sixty-two to sixty-six percent. That market resolves based on closing market cap on the first trading day of whichever company lists second.

The Verge's Hayden Field asks who can actually hold Anthropic to that pacing pledge. Its Long-Term Benefit Trust picks four of seven board seats, but one of those effectively needs the founders' approval. Its decisions also pass through a nominating committee chaired by Daniela Amodei. Tyler Johnston, founder of the watchdog The Midas Project, compares the trust's powers to those of OpenAI's old nonprofit board. He suggests that, hypothetically, a troublesome trustee could simply be hired away. Field notes that a former trustee, Mariano-Florentino Cuéllar, joined Anthropic in August as its Chief Global Affairs Officer. A former employee, who is not named, said the current pace is "untenable under normal market structures."

Human control also made it into Shenzhen. The first summit between Xi, Putin and Trump produced a peace declaration that, the Guardian reports, says nothing about the wars in Ukraine or Iran. It repeats that "a nuclear war cannot be won and must not be fought" but offers no framework for reducing arsenals. It also warns courts like the International Criminal Court not to abuse their jurisdiction, a clause that helps Putin, who faces that court's arrest warrant. In his bilateral with Trump, Xi called for AI to "remain under human control at all times," without any detail. In Berlin, Jared Kushner said Trump believes "peace is achievable" and that Moscow is now "comfortable" with Article Five-style guarantees for Ukraine. Zelensky replied that "Putin has always said no." Drones hit central Kyiv the same day. Polymarket's ceasefire odds slipped from twelve to ten percent. That market resolves "Yes" if there is a ceasefire agreement between Russia and Ukraine by December thirty-first, eleven fifty-nine p.m. Eastern Time.

Nvidia's troubles have a new front. California Attorney General Rob Bonta, already suing OpenAI, has subpoenaed Nvidia. He is investigating whether the company's or Jensen Huang's actions let China obtain advanced chips or confidential information, and whether investors were misled. The stock is near one hundred fifty dollars, almost forty percent below its October peak. Huang testifies to three House committees on December first. Polymarket now gives fifteen percent for a new chief executive by year's end, down from eighteen. That market resolves "Yes" if NVIDIA officially announces a successor to Jensen Huang as chief executive by December thirty-first, twenty twenty-six, eleven fifty-nine p.m. Eastern Time. Separately, the odds that the successor comes from outside the company rose to fifty-six percent. That market resolves according to whether the first officially announced successor by December thirty-first, twenty twenty-eight, is an internal or external candidate.

The International Monetary Fund's Kristalina Georgieva says demand for AI hardware is now pushing up prices worldwide. Together with the Middle East energy shock, she expects it to lift global inflation to four point seven percent this year, and she says many economies need tighter monetary policy. A day earlier, the European Central Bank compared AI debt to the dotcom and subprime bubbles.

Briefly, Reuters reports that Meta employees say its Muse agent mixed up conversations across accounts and surfaced other people's bank balances. Meta says employees test versions the public does not see.

And the International Court of Justice has ruled, for the first time, that a state committed genocide: Myanmar, against the Rohingya.

Anthropic's pledge to pace itself now faces its first quarterly earnings, while Huang's testimony on December first will test Nvidia's account of what reached China.
