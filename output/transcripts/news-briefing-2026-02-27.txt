Here is your daily news briefing for Friday, February twenty-eighth.

The high-stakes standoff between the Pentagon and Anthropic has reached a breaking point. Less than twenty-four hours before today’s deadline, Anthropic C.E.O. Dario Amodei formally refused the Department of Defense’s demands for unrestricted access to the company’s models. Amodei stated he "cannot in good conscience" agree to terms that would bypass the company’s safety constitution, specifically regarding lethal autonomous targeting and mass surveillance. This defiance sets the stage for Secretary Pete Hegseth to follow through on threats to designate the lab a "supply chain risk," a move that could effectively blackball Anthropic from federal contracts and force a radical realignment of the American A.I. sector.

In a move that mirrors the aggressive restructuring seen at X under Elon Musk, Jack Dorsey’s fintech firm, Block, has announced it is cutting its workforce by nearly half. Dorsey is laying off over four thousand employees, shrinking the company from ten thousand to six thousand people. He explicitly cited A.I. as the catalyst, arguing that the majority of modern corporate functions can now be handled by automated systems. Dorsey warned that this "A.I. gamble" is not an isolated event, predicting that most major firms will be forced to follow a similar path within the next year to remain competitive.

On the technical front, Microsoft and Perplexity have both unveiled "agentic" systems that move beyond simple chat interfaces. Microsoft is previewing Copilot Tasks, which uses a dedicated cloud-based browser to complete "busywork" in the background, such as scheduling and data entry, without taxing the user's local device. Simultaneously, Perplexity has launched a feature called "Computer," a high-level agent designed to coordinate and assign work to other specialized A.I. agents. This shift toward "A.I. orchestration" suggests that the industry is moving rapidly toward a future where users manage fleets of digital workers rather than interacting with a single assistant.

Google is also tightening its grip on the physical A.I. landscape by folding its robotics "moonshot," Intrinsic, back into its core business. After five years as an independent Alphabet unit, Intrinsic—often called the "Android of robotics"—will now be integrated directly into Google’s A.I. development. This move signals that Google views physical embodiment and industrial automation as essential to its near-term hardware strategy. Additionally, Google has released "Nano Banana Two," a faster, more capable image generation model, making it the default for both free and pro users in the Gemini ecosystem.

In geopolitics, indirect nuclear talks between the United States and Iran in Geneva have concluded with what mediators call "significant progress." While a final deal remains elusive, the report suggests a potential "de-escalation" that could prevent the immediate military strikes threatened by the White House earlier this week. Meanwhile, in the corporate world, the media landscape has been reshaped as Netflix withdrew its bid for Warner Brothers Discovery. This clears the path for the David Ellison-owned Paramount to acquire the studio, bringing HBO and C.N.N. under a single massive umbrella.

As we look toward the weekend, the tech industry is bracing for the fallout of Anthropic’s refusal to meet the Pentagon’s ultimatum and the resulting impact on national security policy.
