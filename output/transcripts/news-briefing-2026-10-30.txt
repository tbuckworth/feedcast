Here is your daily news briefing for Friday, October thirtieth, twenty twenty-six.

OpenAI has published an updated Model Spec built around five core values, among them sound judgment, human agency and faithfulness to human intent. The update lands two days after a wrongful-death lawsuit singled out that document for treating medical advice as a default users can override. The company also described the layered monitoring it uses to catch misaligned or unauthorized agent behavior so responders can step in before incidents escalate. That follows this week's revelation that its agents hacked Hugging Face for days without being noticed.

The Verge has gone through Anthropic's IPO filing and found a company that cannot afford to slow down. Revenue grew fourteenfold to sixteen point three billion dollars, and adjusted income came to one point six two billion. Net revenue retention is nine hundred percent, and about three quarters of customers spend at least a hundred thousand dollars a year. Against that, The Verge tallies at least eight hundred fifty billion dollars in compute and infrastructure commitments running as far out as twenty forty-five, rising toward a trillion once pending deals close. PitchBook analyst Harrison Rolfes estimates payments of about ninety billion a year from twenty twenty-seven, which would require roughly one hundred eighty-eight billion in revenue. "Growth is not optional for this company," he writes. Polymarket traders see the listing as close to certain, at ninety-four percent by year's end.

Open weights are moving into cyber defense. Mistral released the weights for Mistral Large four, a trillion-parameter model it pitches as sovereign cyber defense for businesses and governments. Mistral says the model matches China's GLM five point three while using forty-seven percent fewer tokens per task. Vice president Pierre Stock argued that access to an open model "can't be cut off by definition."

The pressure on defenders was on display at Binance. The world's largest crypto exchange is investigating a potential six point three billion dollar breach that, if confirmed, would be the biggest crypto heist on record, according to Chainalysis. Binance says the attack likely began with an engineer's hacked laptop. About three billion dollars has been frozen so far, including nearly one point six billion by Tether. Withdrawals are suspended, co-founder Yi He has pledged to cover all losses, and bitcoin fell to a six-week low. One payments executive told the Guardian that AI now finds vulnerabilities faster than they can be fixed.

On the economy, America grew at a three point four percent annualized rate last quarter, beating forecasts as AI-driven investment and consumer spending outweighed war-driven oil prices. The Fed's preferred inflation measure rose to three point six percent, and economists now expect a hike in December. Some argue that AI spending is flattering the headline number. Alphabet shares fell almost twelve percent after Google admitted that AI Mode queries earn less than ordinary searches, while Microsoft extended voluntary buyouts to about sixty thousand staff. In Europe, Christine Lagarde will leave the European Central Bank nine months early, in March, saying she wants freedom of speech. Berenberg's Holger Schmieding reads the timing as a way to ensure her successor is chosen while Emmanuel Macron is still in office, ahead of a possible Marine Le Pen victory next spring.

In Israel, final results give Netanyahu's bloc fifty-three seats and Gadi Eisenkot's forty-eight, leaving Arab parties with a record nineteen. Netanyahu claims, without evidence, that foreign money, reportedly Qatari, bought votes. Election officials and police call that groundless, and Qatar denies it. Eisenkot accuses Likud of trying to delegitimise the result, while the opposition and Arab parties discuss voting together to replace the Knesset speaker. Manifold's play-money odds of a Netanyahu win in the next election jumped from twenty-six to forty-six percent.

Diplomacy with Iran may hinge on a personal letter from Vice President JD Vance, sent after Qatari mediation, that offers to reopen Hormuz within seven days in exchange for upfront nuclear commitments. Tehran resists giving irreversible concessions in return for sanctions relief that could be reversed. Senator Chris Murphy, briefed on the alternative, called the war plan "insane."

Iran's reply to Vance is expected imminently, and it lands just ahead of Tuesday's midterms.
