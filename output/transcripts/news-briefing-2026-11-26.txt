Here is your daily news briefing for Thursday, November twenty-sixth, twenty twenty-six.

Dario Amodei, Anthropic's chief executive, has rejected claims that his company wants humanity to "surrender human free will" to AI, calling the idea "absurd." TechCrunch reports that in a long post on X he wrote that "turning over governance to AI is a bad idea." He suggested drawing a line at recursive self-improvement, meaning AI that improves itself without human help, and wrote that "we may want limits on this process." The pushback follows remarks from rivals. Sam Altman of OpenAI has spoken of keeping "agency over the future." Meta's chief AI officer, Alexandr Wang, wrote that society should not accept a "handover" to AI, and that capability progress is "likely solved." Amodei accused competitors of "vibes-based" positioning, noting that every lab ships ever more capable automation. The tension is real at his own company. Anthropic said in June that Claude wrote more than eighty percent of its merged code. The safety nonprofit METR found that Anthropic's models are improving at about twice their historical rate, driven mainly by AI-assisted research. Anthropic's listing filings call this process "closing the loop." Amodei maintains that it would not be safe for Claude to build frontier models without "meaningful human supervision or understanding."

Nvidia's governance troubles now carry a price. S&P Global Ratings is considering cutting Nvidia from AA to AA-minus, citing "weak executive oversight," missing succession planning and "key man risk." Because Nvidia guarantees debt for neoclouds and infrastructure firms, a downgrade could raise their costs. That includes its planned five hundred billion dollar financing package, where lenders were already demanding bigger guarantees. TechCrunch reports that the central question for investigators is still what Nvidia knew about where Megaspeed's chips ended up. Attorney General Todd Blanche has suggested Nvidia is not a target. But California's attorney general and Congress are pressing ahead, and the Securities and Exchange Commission is reportedly investigating. Three House committees will privately interview Huang and Nvidia's top lawyer next week, before the Senate Banking Committee's public hearing on December third. Senator Elizabeth Warren wants the White House to explain what "input" restored Huang. Nvidia fell another four percent on Wednesday. Polymarket gives it eight percent to be the world's largest company at year's end. That market will resolve to the largest company in the world by market cap on December thirty-first, twenty twenty-six, as of market close.

On trade, the United States trade representative has proposed new tariffs on fourteen partners, including the European Union, Japan, Taiwan and South Korea. China would face an extra twenty percent, bringing its total to about forty. This time the legal basis is section three hundred one of the Trade Act, after the Supreme Court struck down the earlier emergency tariffs. Hearings are set for January twenty-fifth. Inflation is moving the wrong way. The Guardian reports that consumer prices rose three point eight percent annualised in October, the highest since May. That is the last reading before a Fed meeting where officials are widely expected to raise rates. Polymarket's odds of a twenty-five basis point hike in January have slipped from sixty-four to fifty-five percent over the week. That market will resolve to the amount of basis points the upper bound of the target federal funds rate is changed by versus the level it was prior to the Federal Reserve's January twenty twenty-seven meeting.

In privacy news, forty-two state attorneys general are trying to stop bankrupt Character.AI from auctioning chat histories from more than twenty-one million American accounts, over two million of them belonging to minors. The only disclosed bidder is a Delaware company formed on November thirteenth, and the auction is set for December first. Separately, New York has banned AI chatbot toys for five years, after California.

Finally, the Federal Communications Commission has conditionally authorised SpaceX to deploy up to one million satellites designed as orbital data centers. That would increase the number of satellites in orbit roughly fifty-fold, though their technical and economic viability remains unproven.

Next week, Huang faces Congress just as the Character.AI auction tests whether bankruptcy can override a privacy promise.

On the prediction markets, Polymarket's odds that Anthropic announces a Millennium Prize solution this year rose from fifty-five to seventy-one percent. BeInCrypto reported a Claude-authored paper claiming to resolve VP versus VNP, a twin of the P versus NP problem, though the link to the market is inferred from timing. That market will resolve to "Yes" if Anthropic, or an official representative of Anthropic, announces that Anthropic has solved any of the qualifying Millennium Prize Problems by eleven fifty-nine p.m. Eastern Time of the date specified in the title.
