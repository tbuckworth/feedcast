Here is your daily news briefing for Friday, July eighteenth, two thousand twenty-six.

In geopolitics and global security, the maritime conflict in the Middle East has entered its second week of kinetic engagement as the United States conducted air strikes against Iran for the seventh consecutive night. United States Central Command confirmed the strikes were targeted at degrading Iranian armed forces near the Strait of Hormuz, where local reports indicate significant explosions. Simultaneously, the security of global shipping faces a secondary threat as a second commercial tanker in three months has been hijacked off the coast of Yemen by suspected Somali pirates, signaling a major resurgence of piracy in the region.

In artificial intelligence policy and platform safety, San Francisco City Attorney David Chiu has formally ordered Apple and Google to purge "nudify" applications from their respective app stores. The order alleges that both companies have profited from fees generated by these deepfake services, which use AI to digitally remove clothing from photos, in violation of California law. On the consumer side, TikTok has begun testing an opt-in "likeness detection" tool for creators in the United States, allowing them to scan for and report unauthorized AI-generated clones of their identity, a move that mirrors similar safety initiatives recently announced by YouTube.

In AI hardware and corporate litigation, the legal battle between Apple and OpenAI has intensified, with new analysis of the trade secret complaint suggesting it may be a strategic attempt to derail OpenAI’s reported plans for an initial public offering. The lawsuit alleges that over four hundred former Apple employees now work at OpenAI and claims the lab’s hardware division facilitated a pattern of intellectual property theft. As OpenAI faces these legal hurdles, it is also attempting to professionalize its industrial impact; Chief Financial Officer Sarah Friar introduced a new enterprise "scorecard" today, designed to move the industry away from hype by measuring AI performance through "return on compute" and cost per successful task.

In infrastructure and energy, a massive magnitude seven-point-three earthquake has struck off the coast of Mexico, triggering small tsunami waves and adding fresh pressure to North American supply chains already strained by wildfires. The smoke from these fires has become a major diplomatic flashpoint, as President Trump today threatened to impose new tariffs on Canada, accusing the nation of sending "filthy air" into United States cities. In a rare intersection of tech and disaster response, Google-backed satellites under the FireSat program were successfully launched today, specifically designed to detect small-scale wildfires that current orbital sensors often miss.

In the corporate sector, Databricks has reached a staggering one-hundred-and-eighty-eight billion dollar valuation, cementing its status as the leading provider of data infrastructure for the generative AI era. Meanwhile, Amazon is working to resolve a critical bug in its Web Services platform after some cloud customers were erroneously issued bill estimates totaling billions of dollars. In the automotive sector, Amazon’s robotaxi unit, Zoox, has issued a software recall after its vehicles became "confused" and interfered with first responders during heavy smoke conditions—a move that follows a broader warning from federal safety regulators regarding autonomous vehicle behavior in emergency zones.

Expect increased diplomatic tension through the weekend as Canada responds to the United States tariff threats amidst ongoing environmental and seismic disruptions.
