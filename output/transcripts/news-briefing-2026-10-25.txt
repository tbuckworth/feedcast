Here is your daily news briefing for Sunday, October twenty-fifth, twenty twenty-six.

With the midterms nine days away, a new study suggests the models behind America's chatbots are not politically neutral. Convovo, a research studio led by Cornell professor Tapan Parikh, asked models through their developer interfaces to name one candidate in nearly five hundred races. TechCrunch reports that SpaceXAI's Grok four point seven picked the Republican ninety-two percent of the time, while OpenAI's GPT-six point one Sol picked the Democrat ninety-seven percent of the time. The two agreed in fewer than one in ten races. The consumer apps refused to answer, and OpenAI says that refusal reflects how most people actually use ChatGPT. Parikh, though, says he could jailbreak the apps with simple prompts. With web search switched off, the picture split. GPT still chose the Democrat in twenty-four of thirty sample races, while Grok declined in twenty-seven. Parikh concludes GPT's lean is "in the weights," while Grok's comes from the sources and search terms it uses, which included a Christian conservative voter guide in thirty-nine percent of answers. He also flagged about twelve hundred forty unseen tokens added to every Grok request, which he calls potentially "hidden instructions." SpaceXAI did not comment.

Lawmakers are starting to respond to AI in political and commercial messaging. Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro signed a law requiring labels on any ad significantly modified or generated by AI, in the same format as the ad itself. It applies wherever the ad was made, so national campaigns may carry labels elsewhere too. The Verge notes broad loopholes, including undefined terms like "significantly modified" and carve-outs for satire and anything "evidently fictional."

TechCrunch's postmortem on the PHP flaws adds nuance to the AI angle. The bugs, dubbed PHP2Shell, were first spotted in twenty twenty-four but were judged unexploitable and left unpatched. The firm Calif, working within OpenAI and Wiz's Hack the Planet initiative, says AI assisted with the exploit, but most of the work rested on the researchers' own deep knowledge of PHP. In Calif's words, "No model is simply capable of unearthing 'all bugs' with simple prompts. At least for now." Coordinated early patching largely averted mass exploitation, though Amazon says it saw attacks about a week before fixes shipped. Researchers warn many systems remain exposed.

In Ukraine, the military says its Starlink connection is stable again, ten days after speed caps of one hundred sixty kilometres per hour wrecked missions against jet-powered Shaheds. Zelenskyy thanked Trump and the State Department. Hours later, Elon Musk claimed Ukraine had violated Starlink's terms by using it for offensive drone strikes and said that "loophole was closed." A presidential adviser says Ukraine is now "physically unable" to use the system for long- and medium-range strike drones. Separately, the White House says Melania Trump brokered a local ceasefire in occupied Oleshky, where residents have resorted to eating weeds. Kyiv and Moscow both confirmed they will stop firing at one a.m. Greenwich time on October twenty-seventh, so that aid can reach the roughly six thousand people still there and those who wish to leave can be evacuated.

Brazil votes today in a presidential runoff between Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro, whose father is serving a twenty-seven-year sentence for plotting a coup. Polls show a tie after Bolsonaro took the first round forty-seven to forty-five, and no first-round loser has come back since democracy returned in the nineteen-eighties. Rio's City Hall put Saturday's Copacabana rally at about one point one million people. Bettors have moved the other way: Manifold's play-money odds of a Lula win slipped from twenty-four to nineteen percent this week.

In France, the government says at least four hundred thousand people marched in support of striking schoolchildren and against heavy policing, while unions put the figure at double that. Masked groups clashed with police at the end of the Paris march, and the interior ministry reported almost a thousand arrests. Still, the day was calmer than last weekend, and Polymarket odds of a state of emergency by month's end fell from ten to six percent.

Brazil's result tonight, then Britain's budget and Israel's election both on Wednesday, make for a crowded week ahead.
