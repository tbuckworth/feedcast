Here is your daily news briefing for Saturday, July twenty-sixth, two thousand twenty-six.

In space and heavy infrastructure, SpaceX has concluded the thirteenth flight of Starship with an "auspicious" finish that has cleared the way for a tower catch attempt on the next mission. Following the successful reentry of the upper stage, the company aims to return the vehicle directly to the launch pad for a mechanical capture, a critical milestone for rapid reuse. However, as launch cadences increase, the ground infrastructure supporting AI and aerospace is showing signs of fragility. A single fallen power line in Northern Virginia recently exposed systemic vulnerabilities in how data centers respond to grid disruptions. Technical audits revealed that localized failures can cascade through high-density clusters, prompting calls for more resilient, decentralized energy storage as the industry faces massive power requirements.

In artificial intelligence and the labor market, Monday-dot-com has become the latest in a string of twenty major tech firms to officially cite artificial intelligence as a primary driver for layoffs. The company joins a list that includes Patreon and Google in restructuring workforces to prioritize automated workflows over human-led operations. This trend is sparking a cultural backlash; libraries across the United States are seeing record attendance for "Avoiding AI" workshops. These sessions, driven by a growing segment of the public "fed up" with the ubiquity of large language models, focus on protecting data privacy and navigating the internet without encountering algorithmic synthesis.

In geopolitics and environmental crisis, the European climate emergency is worsening as more than three hundred thousand people have been forced to flee their homes across Spain and France. Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez warned of "complex hours" ahead as record-shattering wildfires threaten major population centers and critical infrastructure. The situation is particularly dire near Bordeaux, where French authorities admit they are far from bringing the blazes under control. These fires follow the recent evacuation of NASA’s Deep Space Network complex in Spain, highlighting how extreme weather remains the primary threat to humanity’s most sensitive technical installations.

In global policy and regulation, the European Union is intensifying its conflict with Silicon Valley as researchers formally accuse TikTok, X, and Meta of stonewalling. Under the Digital Services Act, these platforms are legally required to provide data access for independent audits; however, researchers claim the firms are intentionally withholding information to obscure the societal impacts of their algorithms. This follows record-breaking fines against Google and Meta earlier this week, signaling that Brussels is moving from financial penalties to a more aggressive oversight of the data itself.

In regional politics, India is seeing a rare win for organized youth protest as the Education Minister has resigned following weeks of pressure from the "Cockroach Janta Party." The protest, which became the most visible expression of public anger against Prime Minister Narendra Modi’s administration, has officially been called off after successfully forcing leadership changes in the wake of national education and testing scandals.

Expect continued tension in the media sector as Kalshi demands that Netflix remove its "Prediction Games" documentary trailer, alleging defamation and the use of fabricated documents to misrepresent the prediction market industry.
