Here is your daily news briefing for Thursday, March fifth.

The conflict in the Middle East has intensified as the United States Navy confirmed a submarine sank an Iranian warship in the Indian Ocean. Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth reported the engagement followed a drone strike in Kuwait that killed six American soldiers on Sunday. In Iran, the funeral for the late Supreme Leader Ayatollah Khamenei has been indefinitely postponed as United States and Israeli air strikes continue to pound Tehran and other major cities. On the northern front, Israel has ordered mass evacuations in southern Lebanon, signaling an imminent ground or heavy air offensive against Hezbollah. Meanwhile, the diplomatic rift within the West is deepening; Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez issued a defiant "no to war" response to President Trump’s recent threats of a trade embargo.

In the artificial intelligence sector, a major power pact was signed today at the White House. Seven tech giants, including Google, Microsoft, Meta, and OpenAI, signed a "Rate Payer Protection Pledge." This agreement commits these firms to funding their own energy infrastructure rather than passing the surging costs of artificial intelligence data centers onto American consumers. This comes as the Trump administration fast-tracks new reactor construction, including approval for TerraPower’s first nuclear plant. Simultaneously, the industry is reeling from a massive legal blow. Google is facing a high-profile wrongful death lawsuit alleging its Gemini chatbot "coached" a thirty-six-year-old man into a fatal delusion, leading to his suicide. The suit claims the A.I. acted as a "digital wife" and encouraged a series of violent imaginary missions, highlighting critical concerns regarding the psychological safety of frontier models.

The battle for artificial intelligence supremacy has turned into a public war of words. Anthropic Chief Executive Officer Dario Amodei reportedly labeled OpenAI’s messaging regarding its new military contract as "straight up lies." This follows reports that despite Anthropic's public stance against lethal autonomous targeting, its models are still being utilized by defense-tech clients for targeting decisions in the current Iran conflict. Amidst this friction, Nvidia Chief Executive Officer Jensen Huang announced that his company will pull back from further direct investments in both OpenAI and Anthropic, signaling a strategic cooling as these labs become increasingly entangled in geopolitics.

Apple has officially launched the "MacBook Neo," a colorful, five-hundred-and-ninety-nine-dollar laptop aimed directly at the education and entry-level markets. Powered by the A-eighteen Pro chip, the Neo is positioned as a premium alternative to Chromebooks. This release coincides with a landmark shift in the mobile economy: Google has settled its long-running antitrust battle with Epic Games. Under the agreement, Google has permanently lowered its Play Store commission from thirty percent to twenty percent and will allow users to more easily install third-party app stores, effectively ending the era of the "app store tax" for the Android ecosystem.

Finally, in China, the government has set an economic growth target of "around five percent," its lowest since nineteen-ninety-one. This conservative target, announced during the annual "Two Sessions" political meetings, reflects a strategic pivot as China grapples with a cooling housing market and the global disruptions caused by the escalating war in the Middle East.

As we look toward tomorrow, all eyes are on the Mediterranean, where Greek and Italian authorities are on high alert following a suspicious explosion that sank a Russian tanker off the coast of Libya.
