Here is your daily news briefing for Friday, April twenty-fifth.

OpenAI has launched GPT-five-point-five, a surprise successor to the model it released just last month. The company characterizes this latest iteration as its smartest and most intuitive model to date, specifically optimized for complex research, data analysis, and coding. Alongside the model, OpenAI unveiled “Codex,” a new workspace environment designed to transition ChatGPT from a chat interface into an autonomous "super app." Codex allows users to set schedules and triggers for recurring workflows, effectively turning AI threads into persistent agents that can generate reports and dashboards across third-party enterprise tools without manual intervention.

In a direct response to this push for autonomy, Anthropic is expanding its integration into the personal sphere. Claude users can now connect the assistant directly to consumer applications including Spotify, Uber, and TurboTax. This move aims to match OpenAI’s agentic capabilities while addressing the "money squeeze" currently hitting the sector. Anthropic has recently begun restricting the viral agent tool "OpenClaw" to manage soaring compute costs, signaling that the era of unlimited, free high-end AI agents is likely coming to an end. 

The competitive landscape is seeing a brutal re-alignment as Meta announced plans to lay off ten percent of its workforce, approximately eight thousand employees, this May. Despite billionaire Zuckerberg’s pivot to AI, the company is facing the reality of a massive capital expenditure spike. Microsoft is taking a different approach to headcount reduction, offering voluntary retirement buyouts to up to seven percent of its United States staff. Simultaneously, Microsoft is launching "Agent Mode" across its Office suite—a more autonomous version of Copilot that handles complex document and spreadsheet tasks independently, a feature the company has nicknamed "vibe working."

Geopolitical tensions over technology have reached a boiling point as the White House issued a memo accusing Chinese firms of "industrial-scale" theft of American AI models. The Trump administration claims Chinese entities are "distilling" frontier models to bypass export controls and develop domestic capabilities. This domestic-first posture is further reflected in the maritime crisis; United States Central Command reports it has now intercepted thirty-three vessels in the Persian Gulf as part of its ongoing blockade of Iran. In a provocative move, the Pentagon released footage of what it claims are masked Iranian forces staging ship seizures, just hours after the vessels were reported missing.

In cybersecurity, the fallout from the Anthropic "Mythos" breach continues to widen. Unauthorized users have reportedly maintained access to the restricted model for weeks, bypassing the safety tiers Anthropic claimed were necessary to prevent a global security catastrophe. Meanwhile, the cloud hosting provider Vercel has confirmed a second, deeper compromise of its systems. The firm admitted that customer data was stolen prior to the widely reported hack earlier this month, suggesting a persistent threat actor may have had long-term access to the deployment pipelines used by thousands of AI startups.

Finally, in a significant shift for the domestic economy, the Trump administration has officially reclassified cannabis as a less dangerous drug under federal law. The move is designed to expand medical research and ease banking restrictions for the industry, marking one of the largest shifts in federal drug policy in decades.

Looking toward next week, attention turns to the United Kingdom as King Charles prepares for a high-stakes visit to Washington that President Trump suggests could "absolutely" repair the fractured diplomatic relationship between the two nations.
