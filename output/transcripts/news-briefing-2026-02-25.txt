Here is your daily news briefing for Wednesday, February twenty-sixth.

The standoff between Anthropic and the Pentagon has reached a critical impasse. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has given the A.I. lab until this Friday to remove specific safety guardrails or face formal penalties. The dispute centers on three words: "any lawful use." While competitors OpenAI and x-A.I. have reportedly agreed to terms allowing the military to use their models for lethal autonomous targeting and cyber warfare, Anthropic continues to resist, citing its internal safety constitution. This friction highlights a growing divide in Washington over whether private A.I. safety protocols should be permitted to supersede national security requirements.

In a legal victory for OpenAI, a federal judge has dismissed a trade secrets lawsuit filed by Elon Musk’s x-A.I. The suit alleged that OpenAI engaged in systematic poaching of employees and the theft of proprietary data. While the judge granted x-A.I. leave to refile with modified claims, the ruling temporarily eases the legal pressure on OpenAI as it continues its aggressive scaling efforts. To manage this growth, the lab has appointed Arvind K.C., a former executive at Google and Meta, as its first Chief People Officer.

The semiconductor and infrastructure landscape is seeing massive shifts as tech giants secure their A.I. supply chains. Meta is reportedly in talks to acquire a ten percent stake in A.M.D. as part of a deal to supply six gigawatts of computing power. Simultaneously, Google has signed a nearly two-gigawatt clean energy deal that includes a massive one-hundred-hour iron-air battery system from Form Energy. This infrastructure is designed to keep data centers running twenty-four-seven, addressing the intermittency issues of wind and solar power that have previously throttled A.I. expansion.

In geopolitics, the security situation in Mexico remains volatile following the death of "El Mencho." Armed groups yesterday rammed a prison gate in Puerto Vallarta, successfully freeing twenty-three inmates. This complicates an already dense regional agenda for the Trump administration, as President Trump today publicly criticized his own generals. According to reports, the President hit back at General Dan Caine, who warned that a military strike against Iran could trigger a prolonged and "unpredictable" regional conflict. With the nuclear ultimatum now expired, the White House continues to message a "patriotic" readiness for escalation.

In markets and enterprise tech, Anthropic has launched "Claude Cowork," a suite of plug-ins that allow its A.I. to take direct actions in Google Workspace, WordPress, and DocuSign. This move toward "agentic" A.I. comes as new data from Stripe indicates that A.I. startups are hitting ten million dollars in annual recurring revenue faster than any previous generation of software companies, with some reaching that milestone within just three months of launch. 

As we look toward tomorrow, the tech industry is bracing for a potential earthquake in the payments sector, as reports intensify that Stripe is exploring a deal to acquire some or all of PayPal.
