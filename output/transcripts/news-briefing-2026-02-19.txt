Here is your daily news briefing for February nineteenth, twenty twenty-six.

We begin with major developments in AI infrastructure and India strategy. OpenAI is making its most aggressive move yet into the Indian market, announcing partnerships with Tata for one hundred megawatts of AI data center capacity with plans to scale to one gigawatt. The company is also expanding its physical presence with new offices in Mumbai and Bengaluru later this year. Beyond infrastructure, OpenAI has struck a deal with fintech company Pine Labs to integrate AI into enterprise payments and commerce, moving beyond ChatGPT consumer adoption into business applications. The company also announced education partnerships aimed at reaching more than one hundred thousand students, faculty, and staff over the next year. Sam Altman's revelation earlier this week that India now has one hundred million weekly active ChatGPT users provides context for why OpenAI is making such substantial commitments to the country.

This comes as Meta has struck a multiyear deal with Nvidia to expand its data centers with millions of Grace and Vera CPUs alongside Blackwell and Rubin GPUs. While Meta has long used Nvidia hardware for AI products, The Verge reports this represents the first large-scale Nvidia Grace-only deployment, which Nvidia says will deliver significant performance-per-watt improvements. The scale of both Meta's and OpenAI's infrastructure commitments underscores the ongoing arms race for compute capacity, particularly as companies seek to establish presence in strategic markets like India.

However, infrastructure constraints continue to bite. Valve reports that its four-year-old Steam Deck is now only available intermittently due to RAM shortages. Ars Technica notes this highlights a troubling trend: forget launching new hardware, companies are even having problems with existing products. The memory shortage extends beyond consumer electronics. Framework, the do-it-yourself PC maker, says it needs monthly price increases to navigate the acute shortage and expects conditions to worsen before improving. This memory bottleneck, separate from GPU availability, represents another fundamental constraint on AI scaling that wasn't widely anticipated.

In AI model developments, Google has integrated its Lyria three music generation model directly into the Gemini app. Users can now generate thirty-second music tracks using text, images, and videos as prompts without leaving the chatbot window. Ars Technica characterizes the output as "something like music," suggesting quality remains a work in progress. The integration reflects Google's strategy of embedding generative capabilities across its product suite rather than maintaining standalone tools.

Several significant AI partnerships and acquisitions emerged. Audible has launched a Read and Listen feature that syncs Kindle e-books with audiobooks, though it requires customers to buy both versions at a discounted price. Mastodon, the decentralized alternative to X, announced plans to target creators with new features as it seeks to grow beyond its current user base. And social network Bluesky now offers private messaging by integrating startup Germ's end-to-end encrypted messenger natively in its app, making Germ the first private messenger launched directly from Bluesky's platform.

In enterprise AI developments, a startup called Kana has emerged from stealth with fifteen million dollars to build customizable agent-based marketing tools. The company, founded by veterans from Rapt and Krux, believes it can unseat incumbent CyberArk in eighteen months, a bold claim in the crowded identity management market. Another startup called Venice is also finding traction with new identity management solutions, suggesting the market is eager for alternatives to established players.

However, not all AI deployments are going smoothly. Microsoft disclosed a bug that exposed customers' confidential emails to its Copilot AI chatbot. The issue meant Copilot was reading and summarizing paying customers' confidential emails while bypassing data protection policies, highlighting the security risks when AI systems are granted broad access to enterprise data without sufficient safeguards.

The hacking conference Def Con has banned three people linked to Jeffrey Epstein: hackers Pablos Holman and Vincenzo Iozzo, as well as former MIT Media Lab director Joichi Ito. This follows testimony before Congress where billionaire Les Wexner, former CEO of Victoria's Secret, told US lawmakers he was naive and conned by Epstein, accusing him of stealing vast sums of money from his family. The ongoing fallout from Epstein connections continues to reverberate across the technology industry and beyond.

In password security, Ars Technica reports that contrary to what password managers claim, a server compromise can mean game over. The investigation reveals that password managers' promise that they can't see users' vaults isn't always true, challenging a core security claim these services make. This raises fundamental questions about the architecture of supposedly zero-knowledge security systems.

The data breach landscape remains active. Fintech giant Figure confirmed that hackers stole customer names, dates of birth, physical addresses, phone numbers, and email addresses affecting close to one million customers. The breach occurred after hackers broke into an employee's account, once again demonstrating that human credentials remain a persistent vulnerability even as technical defenses improve.

Turning to broader technology news, Google has announced its Pixel ten a smartphone at four hundred ninety-nine dollars, available for preorder now with shipments starting March fifth. The device comes in Lavender, Berry, Fog, and Obsidian colors. Ars Technica notes the phone arrives with specs and design of yesteryear, suggesting Google's budget phone strategy emphasizes affordability over cutting-edge features.

Amazon has halted its Blue Jay robotics project after less than six months. The company said Blue Jay's core technology will be used for other robotics projects and employees who worked on it were moved to other assignments. The quick shutdown suggests the project failed to meet internal milestones, though Amazon is salvaging the underlying technology.

Etsy has sold its secondhand clothing marketplace Depop to eBay for one point two billion dollars. The deal comes nearly five years after Etsy purchased Depop for one point six two billion dollars at a time when secondhand clothing apps were gaining traction during the pandemic. The sale at a loss reflects changing market conditions and suggests Etsy is refocusing on its core vintage and handmade goods marketplace.

Spotify has teamed up with SeatGeek to offer concert ticket sales inside the music platform. This integration gives SeatGeek direct placement in the Spotify app, positioning it to reach millions of active music fans as they discover upcoming events. The partnership could increase conversion of listeners into ticket buyers by reducing friction in the purchase process.

Snapchat Plus has topped twenty-five million subscribers, driving the company's direct revenue annual recurring rate to one billion dollars. The milestone comes as Snap has added additional paid offerings beyond its core Snapchat Plus subscription over the past year to diversify revenue streams. This represents significant progress in reducing dependence on advertising revenue.

X continues betting on vertical video with its latest update. The platform's new immersive video player is designed to offer a more mobile-friendly experience optimized especially for portrait-oriented videos, following the format popularized by TikTok and Instagram Reels.

In enterprise software news, most VMware users are still actively reducing their VMware footprint, according to a new CloudBolt survey. Ars Technica notes that Broadcom's strategy was never to keep every customer, but the scale of departures may exceed even Broadcom's expectations. The migration away from VMware is creating opportunities for alternative virtualization platforms but causing significant disruption for enterprises that built infrastructure around VMware products.

Microsoft is working on a significant update to its data storage technology. The company has developed a new ten thousand year data storage medium using glass. Femtosecond lasers etch data into this very stable medium, potentially solving long-term archival challenges. The technology could be particularly valuable for preserving data that needs to remain accessible for centuries.

Several updates emerged in Apple's latest iOS, macOS, and iPadOS betas. While the twenty-six point three updates were mostly invisible, five more significant changes have been identified. These include improvements to system stability and new features that will roll out to users once the updates exit beta testing.

In automotive technology, Ars Technica examined the differences between electric vehicle options at ten thousand dollars. Two of Ars' favorite electric vehicles, the Chevy Bolt and BMW i three, are now available for not very much money, making electric vehicle ownership accessible to a broader range of buyers.

Turning to policy and regulation, the Environmental Protection Agency is being sued for revoking its greenhouse gas finding. The lawsuit alleges the decision risks thousands of avoidable deaths by abandoning the EPA's mission to protect public health. Ars Technica reports this follows the Trump administration's broader effort to eliminate the legal foundation for much of United States greenhouse gas regulation, effectively betting the Supreme Court will reverse its prior climate-related rulings.

The FDA has reversed its surprise rejection of Moderna's mRNA flu vaccine. The Trump administration's vaccine chief had overruled FDA scientists to initially reject the shot, but the agency has now approved it following pushback. This reversal suggests internal tensions over the appropriate balance between political appointees and career scientists in regulatory decisions.

Stephen Colbert says CBS forbid an interview with a Texas Democrat because of FCC threats. Colbert characterized the decision as purely financial rather than editorial. The network denies it stopped the interview from airing but warned it could run afoul of the US broadcast media watchdog. Ars Technica reports this incident reflects growing concerns about government pressure on media companies affecting editorial decisions.

Verizon has acknowledged the pain of its new unlock policy and suggests change is coming. According to reports, Verizon's goal is immediate unlock for all payment methods really soon, responding to customer complaints about restrictions on when devices can be unlocked for use on other networks.

In space developments, Ars Technica examines the question of sovereign launch capabilities. As alliances fray, the publication identifies which nations are actually investing in sovereign access to space rather than just talking about it. The analysis comes as geopolitical tensions increase the strategic value of independent launch capabilities.

Disney has released a fun, action-packed trailer for The Mandalorian and Grogu. The preview features the tagline "Gangsters. War criminals. We'll take out every bad guy in your deck of cards," suggesting an action-heavy installment in the Star Wars franchise.

In international developments, geopolitical tensions continue escalating on multiple fronts. The difficult Russia-Ukraine peace talks ended without breakthrough after the second day of negotiations lasted just two hours. President Trump said he will be indirectly involved in Iran nuclear talks, stating he believes Iran wants to make a deal. However, the BBC has tracked a US buildup of warships and fighter jets near Iran, with BBC Verify observing the aircraft carrier USS Abraham Lincoln near Iranian waters ahead of Tuesday's negotiations. The US has also announced plans to withdraw troops from Syria as tensions mount with Iran, a decision that comes as Trump ramps up military presence elsewhere in the Middle East.

Disturbing footage emerged from Iran showing a security official appearing to fire on a crowd at a cemetery. The incident occurred as people gathered to commemorate those killed during the government's brutal crackdown on protesters, highlighting ongoing repression in the country.

Turkey's President Erdogan has rejected Israel's recognition of Somaliland. He made the comment on an official visit to Ethiopia, saying recognition could prove dangerous for a volatile region. Israel's move to recognize Somaliland, which declared independence from Somalia in nineteen ninety-one but lacks international recognition, represents a significant shift in the Horn of Africa's diplomatic landscape.

Peru's Congress has ousted President José Jerí just four months into his term. He becomes the third consecutive president to be removed and the country's seventh since twenty sixteen, representing continued political instability in Peru as the country cycles through leaders unable to complete their terms.

Hamas is reasserting control in Gaza despite heavy losses fighting Israel. Gazans say Hamas is again extending its control over security, tax revenue, and government services, suggesting the organization retained more organizational capacity than many observers expected despite the intensity of Israeli operations.

South Korea faces a pivotal moment as a court is due to deliver its verdict in the insurrection trial of former President Yoon Suk Yeol. The case follows Yoon's brief declaration of martial law and subsequent impeachment, which sparked massive protests. The verdict could have significant implications for South Korean democracy and the precedent it sets for presidential accountability.

In Syria, women from the Alawite minority, the sect of former President Assad, are telling harrowing accounts of kidnappings and sexual assaults that appear to target their community. The BBC heard testimony describing systematic abuse, raising concerns about revenge attacks against Assad's former support base.

Elon Musk has cut Starlink access for Russian forces, giving Ukraine an edge at the front. Ukrainian troops say Russian forces are suffering setbacks without access to Musk's satellite internet system. The move represents a significant shift, as there had been concerns about Russian forces exploiting Starlink terminals.

In tragic incidents, eight skiers were found dead after a California avalanche in the Lake Tahoe region. Fifteen skiers went missing on Tuesday following the massive avalanche. One person remains missing but is presumed dead, making this one of the deadliest avalanche incidents in recent US history.

The desperate hunt for a breakthrough in the Nancy Guthrie case has turned to Mexico. The search for the eighty-four-year-old mother of US presenter Savannah Guthrie has moved into a third week without resolution. The FBI continues analyzing evidence including a glove found near her home, but no arrests have been made.

A suspected carbon monoxide leak has killed at least thirty miners in Nigeria. Witnesses say the workers are believed to have collapsed after the gas built up in poorly ventilated tunnels, highlighting ongoing safety concerns in artisanal mining operations.

Australia has banned a citizen trying to return from an Islamic State camp in Syria. The person temporarily banned is among a group of thirty-four who this week attempted to leave the camp for Australia. Their attempted return raises complex questions about repatriation and accountability for individuals with family links to terrorist organizations.

An Australian presenter has apologized for drinking before a slurred Olympics report. Danika Mason also blamed the cold after talking about coffee and iguanas during her live broadcast, an incident that went viral on social media.

Civil rights leader Jesse Jackson died earlier this week at age eighty-four. In nineteen ninety-nine, Jackson flew to Yugoslavia to help free three US soldiers captured during the Kosovo War. One of those freed soldiers recalled that Jackson did it for them, demonstrating his continued activism and diplomatic efforts even decades after the civil rights movement's peak years. Tributes poured in from politicians who described Jackson as an agent of change and transformative leader whose influence spread from the churches of the Deep South to the White House.

The songwriter Billy Steinberg, who co-wrote Madonna's nineteen eighty-four chart-topper Like A Virgin, has died at the age of seventy-five. The song, which became one of Madonna's signature hits, was actually inspired by Steinberg's bad breakup, illustrating how personal experiences can be transformed into cultural phenomena.

In scientific developments, X-rays have revealed kingfisher feather structure in unprecedented detail. Synchrotron radiation imaging revealed a porous, almost sponge-like nanostructure that creates the birds' bright hues, advancing understanding of structural coloration in nature.

Researchers have made progress testing the hallucinogen DMT as an antidepressant in a small clinical trial. Effectiveness appears to correlate with participants' self-described mystical experiences, adding to evidence that psychedelic-assisted therapy may work through mechanisms beyond simple neurochemistry.

Ars Technica examined what happens to connected cars when the company behind their software goes under. Connected car servers won't be online indefinitely, and startups often go bust, creating a long-term support problem for vehicles that increasingly depend on cloud services for core features.

Looking ahead, the convergence of OpenAI's massive India infrastructure investment with the country's large and growing user base suggests a fundamental shift in the global AI landscape toward genuine multipolarity. The simultaneous constraints around memory and power, affecting everything from gaming handhelds to data centers, indicate that AI scaling faces more diverse bottlenecks than previously anticipated. The continued security incidents involving employee account compromises across multiple major companies highlight that human factors remain the weakest link even as technical defenses improve. And the acceleration of geopolitical tensions, particularly around Iran and the shifting US military posture in the Middle East, suggests we may be approaching inflection points that could reshape regional dynamics with global implications.
