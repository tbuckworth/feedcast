Here is your daily news briefing for Wednesday, November eleventh, twenty twenty-six.

New York is gearing up to enforce one of the country's most significant AI safety laws. TechCrunch reports that the state opened a registration portal on Tuesday, and frontier developers with more than five hundred million dollars in annual revenue, including Anthropic and OpenAI, must sign up by January first. Under the RAISE Act, which kicks in at the start of the year, they must publish safety frameworks, report critical safety incidents within seventy-two hours, and issue public model transparency reports. A new state office will enforce it, with fines of up to one million dollars for a first violation and three million for later ones. Governor Kathy Hochul also launched a state AI research centre at Binghamton University to independently evaluate advanced systems and review the companies' frameworks. "They tell us get on board or get left behind," Hochul said of tech leaders, accusing them of racing "to the AI cliff." New York's seventy-two-hour reporting window is far tighter than the fifteen days California allows.

Google is back at the frontier. It released Gemini 4 Carbon to the public, two weeks after giving it to a select group of cybersecurity defenders, and calls it its strongest model yet. The Verge says Google's own benchmarks put it behind only Anthropic's Fable 5.2 on most metrics, with the usual caveat about cherry-picked results. Introductory pricing is two dollars per million input tokens, roughly a quarter of what Fable 5.2 costs. Even after that price doubles at month's end, it will undercut its rivals. Google is also previewing a "swarm" feature that turns a single prompt into a coordinated team of agents. Polymarket traders now give Google seventy-four percent odds of holding the best model at year's end, up from fifty-six a week ago. Separately, DeepMind unveiled Gemini Robotics 2.5. Apptronik humanoids running it hit a ninety-six percent success rate kitting parts in Mercedes-Benz factories. DeepMind director Kanishka Rao called closing the last four percent a "steep road."

There is more detail on the AI-driven proof of the Aaronson-Ambainis conjecture. A researcher at the startup Physical Superintelligence, David Turturean, used early access to Fable 5.2 to settle the eighteen-year-old quantum computing problem. Scott Aaronson, one of the two researchers who posed it, wrote that he doesn't fully understand the proof. He accepts it because it was formalized in Lean and checked by top researchers. He described feeling "satisfied, awestruck, disappointed, and fatigued." Turturean told TechCrunch this "might be the last paper I ever put out." The company's chief executive, Matt Pines, says theoretical physics is on its way to becoming "mostly the domain of machines."

Anthropic's amended filing sets a price range of seventy to seventy-eight dollars a share. At the top of that range, the company would be valued at one point four trillion dollars on a fully diluted basis, well short of the two trillion once expected. Counting a private placement with Alphabet and Nvidia and a possible greenshoe, it could raise about one hundred billion dollars. That would beat SpaceX's record raise. The filing puts Anthropic's revenue run rate at eighty-one billion dollars, up ten billion in a month, against more than four hundred fifty billion in compute commitments by twenty thirty. Dario Amodei and each of his six co-founders will sell about two hundred sixty million dollars of shares. CNBC reports trading is expected on November nineteenth, and Polymarket puts the chance of a listing this year at ninety-seven percent.

Nvidia's crisis has reached the White House. President Trump says the FBI is investigating Jensen Huang's affair with Alice Huang, the alleged Chinese spy. Trump says Huang told him he "had no idea she was a spy," and that he believes him. Beijing calls the allegations "entirely groundless." Nvidia lost about two hundred fifty billion dollars in value on Monday and now faces a shareholder class action alleging it concealed the relationship.

On Iran, Trump says the war is "effectively over," adding that the United States remains "locked and loaded." Brent crude fell to its lowest level since August. American October inflation came in at zero point four percent for the month, and a Fed rate hike in December is still expected. In Israel, the opposition moved to seize the Knesset speakership and filed bills on prime-ministerial term limits. Polymarket's odds that Netanyahu is out by year's end have jumped to fifty-four percent.

Next week brings Anthropic's pricing, Huang's congressional briefing and a Knesset speaker vote, all within days of each other.

On the prediction markets, Polymarket's odds that Anthropic's valuation hits one point seven five trillion dollars by year's end fell from fifty-seven to thirty-six percent. Fortune attributes the drop to the new price range, which implies at most one point four trillion. Odds of an Anthropic listing on November nineteenth jumped from thirty-one to seventy-six percent after CNBC reported that trading is expected on that date.
