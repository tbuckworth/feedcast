Here is your daily news briefing for Thursday, November twelfth, twenty twenty-six.

Jensen Huang's position at Nvidia has gone from shaky to precarious. The Financial Times, via Ars Technica, reports the board held urgent talks Wednesday night after news broke of Huang's romantic relationship with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice-president of healthcare and an eighteen-year veteran of the company. That comes days after Huang admitted an affair with an executive at a client whom United States officials believe is a Chinese intelligence asset. The board, which unusually has no chair, had hired a former federal prosecutor to investigate and had so far backed Huang. Its options now include having him step back during the inquiry or pushing for his immediate departure, and Nvidia's governance filings set out no emergency succession plan. Polymarket's odds that Huang's resignation or removal is announced by year's end leapt from sixteen to eighty percent.

On AI capabilities, Google DeepMind had a strong scientific day. Researchers used a new agentic framework called AlphaProof Code to formally verify key cryptographic guarantees of the Signal protocol in just four weeks, Wired reports. The protocol also protects Google Messages and WhatsApp. It found no new vulnerabilities, though run on older Signal versions it correctly flagged known, long-fixed flaws. DeepMind research scientist Swarat Chaudhuri cautioned there is "a long distance to go" before this scales to all the world's software. Separately, DeepMind unveiled AlphaGenome 2, trained on more than one hundred million cell profiles, which predicts how genetic variants affect specific cell types. DeepMind says it identifies nearly twice as many expression-altering variants as its previous model. Genomics lead Žiga Avsec says the cell-level detail matters because drugs target specific cell types. Research scientist Adam Gayoso calls the breakthrough "tying human genetics to cell biology."

On the money side of AI, the picture is mixed. Bloomberg reports OpenAI's quarterly revenue topped ten billion dollars for the first time, nearly fifty percent above the second quarter, with a run rate of fifty billion. TSMC's board approved up to five hundred million dollars for OpenAI preferred stock and five hundred million for Anthropic shares at its IPO price, though analysts quoted by the Wall Street Journal warn that such circular financing muddies real demand. CoreWeave, meanwhile, is leaning on customer prepayments and short-term contracts priced above forty million dollars per megawatt a year, after posting a seven hundred nine million dollar quarterly loss.

On Iran, the Guardian reports that Mehdi Mohammadi, an adviser to chief negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, says Washington has set roughly a two-week window before Trump decides on military action, and that Tehran is preparing a response. Iran rejected a request from Vice President JD Vance for a direct meeting. Officials also warned that American bases in Europe would be targeted if fighting resumes.

Abu Dhabi talks produced the war's largest planned prisoner exchange, over a thousand per side, but no peace. Ukraine's David Arakhamia reported "really big progress" on security guarantees. Russia's Vladimir Medinsky insisted "every inch of our land will be returned" and called for "a new Yalta and Potsdam." Kushner and Witkoff head to Moscow today, then Dublin to meet Zelenskyy. Polymarket's ceasefire odds dipped to ten percent.

In American politics, Democrat Scott Colom unseated Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith in Mississippi, becoming the state's first Black senator elected since Reconstruction and giving Democrats at least fifty-five Senate seats.

With Nvidia's board deciding Huang's fate and Anthropic's pricing days away, the next week will test whether AI's money machine can hold together.

On the prediction markets, Polymarket's odds of an Anthropic IPO valuation between one point five and one point seven five trillion dollars jumped from twenty-five to fifty-five percent. Fortune, citing the Financial Times, reports bankers are considering pricing above the range on strong demand. Kalshi's odds of any company achieving AGI before twenty twenty-eight rose from thirty-eight to fifty-three percent; no clear cause was found.
