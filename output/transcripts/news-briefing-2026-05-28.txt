Here is your daily news briefing for Wednesday, May twenty-eighth.

The fragile diplomatic framework in the Middle East has been shaken by a direct military exchange between the United States and Iran. While ceasefire negotiations continue in Qatar, the United States launched strikes against an Iranian military site today in response to Tehran allegedly targeting an American base. Simultaneously, Israel confirmed it has killed Mohammed Odeh, the new head of Hamas’s military wing, in a Gaza City residential strike. In the north, Israel has declared areas south of Lebanon's Zahrani River as "combat zones," signaling a significant ground expansion. These escalations have caused global oil prices to jump, erasing the market optimism seen earlier this week.

In a landmark move for autonomous finance, Robinhood has announced it will allow users to deploy AI agents to trade stocks. This new feature allows traders to create dedicated accounts where AI can independently execute buys and sells within set capital limits. This represents the first major integration of "agentic" AI into retail capital markets, shifting the technology from an advisory tool to an active market participant with the power to move assets autonomously.

The technology landscape is shifting away from United States-centric manufacturing as Nvidia announced a massive one hundred fifty billion dollar annual investment in Taiwan. This move effectively frames Taiwan as the global "epicenter" of AI infrastructure, a strategic pivot that analysts suggest is a direct rebuff to the Trump administration’s efforts to reshore AI hardware production. While Nvidia doubles down on East Asia, Snowflake has signed a six billion dollar deal with Amazon Web Services to secure custom AI chips, further intensifying the pressure on Nvidia’s dominant market position in the enterprise compute sector.

AI safety and policy have reached a new level of public visibility through a series of interventions and regulatory shifts. Pope Leo the fourteenth formally released his encyclical on AI in the presence of Anthropic’s co-founder, warning that AI-driven processes risk eroding fundamental human rights and status. To counter the rise of synthetic misinformation, YouTube has begun automatically identifying and labeling AI-generated content on its platform. Meanwhile, the specialized coding startup Cognition has reached a twenty-five billion dollar valuation, reporting nearly five hundred million dollars in annualized revenue, signaling that the "full automation" of AI development is becoming a massive commercial reality.

Domestically, the Trump administration is preparing a controversial public health response to the Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo. Reports today indicate plans to block American citizens exposed to the virus from returning to the United States, proposing instead to relocate them to Kenya for quarantine. This comes as the World Health Organization warns of a "catastrophic collision" of disease and conflict in the region, which continues to threaten global cobalt supplies essential for the battery and tech sectors.

Looking toward tomorrow, the Federal Aviation Administration will begin its formal investigation into SpaceX’s Starship booster failure, a process that will keep the heavy-lift vehicle grounded during a critical period for its upcoming initial public offering.
