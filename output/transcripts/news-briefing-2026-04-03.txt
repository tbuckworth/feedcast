Here is your daily news briefing for Thursday, April third.

The second space age has officially accelerated as the four-person crew of Artemis Two transitioned from Earth orbit to a lunar trajectory. For the first time since nineteen seventy-two, humans are now beyond low-Earth orbit, bound for the far side of the Moon. While mission commander Reid Wiseman briefly struggled with orbital connectivity issues—specifically a malfunctioning Outlook email client—NASA reports all critical systems are nominal. This mission is being viewed as the final "traditional" NASA operation before the agency pivots to a heavy reliance on SpaceX and Blue Origin for the planned twenty-twenty-eight lunar landing.

In a significant move to control the narrative surrounding the industry, OpenAI has acquired the "Technical Buffalo Podcast Network," or T-B-P-N. This buzzy, founder-led talk show is known for long-form interviews with elite tech executives, including Sam Altman and Mark Zuckerberg. While OpenAI states the network will remain editorially independent under its new "chief political operative" Chris Lehane, the acquisition signals a direct push by A-I labs to own the media channels that shape public and regulatory perception. Separately, OpenAI introduced more flexible pay-as-you-go pricing for its Enterprise tiers, seeking to lower the barrier for corporate adoption.

The geopolitical strain of the war with Iran is now manifesting in global supply chains and domestic leadership. In Tehran, the Iranian government confirmed that its two largest steel plants have been shuttered following coordinated air strikes, which they attribute to Israel and the United States. The resulting industrial paralysis is rippling outward; in India, manufacturers of glass and plastic packaging warned of imminent price spikes for bottled water and beer due to raw material shortages. Within the White House, the leadership carousel continues as President Trump removed Attorney General Pam Bondi, replacing her with Todd Blanche, the former prosecutor who represented the President in his New York criminal proceedings.

The "agentic" A-I shift is meeting stiff resistance from both the legal system and technical reality. The California-based startup Kintsugi, which spent seven years developing A-I to detect depression and anxiety through speech, has announced its closure after failing to secure Food and Drug Administration clearance. The company will open-source its models, highlighting the extreme regulatory difficulty of moving A-I agents into clinical diagnostics. Meanwhile, a new lawsuit has targeted Perplexity, Google, and Meta, alleging that their "Incognito" or private browsing modes are a "sham" because user data and chat logs are still harvested to increase advertising revenue.

On the security front, researchers have demonstrated a new class of hardware vulnerability called "G-D-D-R-Hammer." By exploiting the memory-refresh patterns of Nvidia graphics cards, attackers can gain complete control over a host machine's central processor. This discovery is particularly significant for data centers where multiple users share powerful G-P-U clusters, as it suggests the isolation between different virtual machines may be fundamentally compromised at the silicon level.

Looking toward tomorrow, maritime markets are bracing for Amazon's new "fuel surcharge," a temporary tax on all third-party sellers designed to offset the rising logistics costs sparked by the conflict in the Middle East.
