Here is your daily news briefing for Friday, March twenty-first.

The Trump administration has unveiled a new seven-point legislative blueprint for artificial intelligence that seeks to centralize control in Washington. The policy would explicitly bar individual states from enacting their own AI regulations, arguing that a fragmented legal landscape hinders the "national strategy for global AI dominance." While the plan includes specific child safety rules, its primary focus is federal preemption of state laws, effectively shifting the burden of digital safety from corporations to parents and federal oversight.

In a significant shift to stabilize the global economy, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has officially lifted sanctions on a portion of Iranian oil. The Treasury Department estimates this move will release approximately one hundred and forty million barrels of oil currently at sea to global markets. This decision follows a period of soaring gas prices and new analysis showing that recent Iranian strikes on bases used by the United States caused over eight hundred million dollars in damages. Despite the easing of oil sanctions, the Department of Justice formally accused the Iranian government today of operating "Handala," the hacking group responsible for the devastating cyberattack on the medical firm Stryker.

In the AI sector, Amazon is reportedly returning to the smartphone market over a decade after the failure of its Fire Phone. Internally code-named "Transformer," the new device is designed to be an AI-centric "Alexa phone." Unlike traditional hardware, reports suggest the device may deprioritize a standard app store in favor of a fluid, agentic interface powered by Amazon’s large language models. This follows a broader trend of "agentic" integration, as WordPress also announced today it will now allow AI agents to independently write and publish entire posts, a move critics worry will further saturate the web with synthetic content.

The boundary between search and original content is also dissolving as Google begins using AI to replace news headlines in search results. Rather than displaying the publisher's original title, Google is testing AI-generated summaries as the primary link text. This comes as a new study indicates a growing cultural "disconnect," where corporate enthusiasm for AI deployment is being met with increasing "no thanks" sentiment from the general public. Adding to the tension, a publisher recently pulled a horror novel from shelves following allegations of unauthorized AI use, marking one of the first major literary recalls of its kind.

Finally, the scale of AI infrastructure is moving into orbit. Jeff Bezos and Blue Origin have announced "Project Sunrise," a plan for a celestial "megaconstellation" consisting of over fifty thousand satellites. Unlike existing communication networks, these satellites are designed to function as space-based data centers, performing high-energy compute tasks off-planet to bypass terrestrial power and cooling bottlenecks. 

Looking toward tomorrow, focus shifts to Nevada, where a court-ordered temporary ban on the prediction market Kalshi is set to take effect, potentially reshaping the legal landscape for high-stakes political wagering.
