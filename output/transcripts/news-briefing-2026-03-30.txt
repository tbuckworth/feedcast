Here is your daily news briefing for Monday, March thirty-first.

The Middle East conflict has escalated into a direct campaign against regional industrial infrastructure. Following weeks of maritime tension, Iranian forces have launched a series of strikes targeting major aluminum production sites in the United Arab Emirates and Bahrain. These attacks mark a significant shift from intercepting tankers to degrading the physical economic assets of Gulf nations. Locally, the human cost of the regional war continues to climb; an Israeli strike in southern Lebanon killed three journalists today, including a prominent reporter for the Hezbollah-affiliated Al-Manar T-V, drawing sharp condemnation from international press freedom groups.

In the artificial intelligence sector, the narrative surrounding OpenAI’s decision to kill its Sora video project is shifting toward data privacy concerns. While initial reports cited a pivot to productivity, new analysis from TechCrunch suggests the project’s closure may have been a preemptive move to avoid legal fallout. Before its cancellation, Sora had invited users to upload their own faces to "star" in AI-generated clips, leading to internal and external alarms regarding a massive biometric data grab. This "reality check" for AI video comes as the broader industry faces a backlash against "slop"—the high-volume generation of synthetic music and art that critics argue is devaluing professional human creative work.

Despite the pullback in video, the "agentic" era of AI is expanding into social media architecture. The team behind the decentralized social network Bluesky has unveiled Attie, a new AI assistant powered by Anthropic’s Claude. Built on the "A-T Protocol," Attie allows users to use natural language to design their own custom algorithms and feed filters. Instead of being beholden to a single corporate black-box algorithm, users can now instruct the AI to curate their experience based on specific technical or personal preferences. This move signals a significant push toward "user-sovereign" social media, where the AI serves the consumer rather than the advertiser.

On the domestic front, the "No Kings" protest movement against the administration has reached a new peak of cultural visibility. Large rallies were held across the United States today, most notably in Minnesota, where Bruce Springsteen performed for crowds protesting the President’s recent executive actions. These demonstrations are coinciding with a period of intense economic pressure; in Australia, the government has been forced to halve fuel taxes and offer free public transport in states like Victoria and Tasmania to prevent total economic paralysis caused by the global energy crunch.

Finally, in a sign of the times for political engagement and forecasting, the prediction market Polymarket has opened a physical "Situation Room" pop-up bar in Washington, D-C. The move reflects the increasing institutionalization of "prop betting" on policy and elections, as sophisticated traders and lobbyists increasingly look to decentralized markets rather than traditional polling to gauge the direction of the United States government.

Looking toward tomorrow, maritime insurers are expected to release updated risk premiums for the Persian Gulf following the latest strikes on industrial sites in the Emirates and Bahrain.
