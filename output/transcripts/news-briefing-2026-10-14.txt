Here is your daily news briefing for Wednesday, October fourteenth, twenty twenty-six.

Britain’s domestic intelligence service has launched an Artificial Intelligence Threat Assessment Centre. In his annual speech, reported by the BBC, intelligence chief Sir Ken McCallum said officers had caught terrorists trying to use artificial intelligence to improve reconnaissance and attack methods, including through drones. He identified extremist use of biological weapons as the biggest potential misuse threat, while stressing that it has not yet materialised. McCallum also said threats from foreign adversaries had quadrupled over the past decade, leaving counterterrorism and state-threat work roughly equal in scale. The new centre will assess emerging risks and distinguish demonstrated capabilities from exaggerated claims, working alongside Britain’s Artificial Intelligence Security Institute.

Google has released Gemini four Argon, its first major frontier model since late twenty twenty-five. According to The Verge, Google claims it outperforms leading OpenAI and Anthropic models across benchmarks covering coding, cybersecurity, science, finance and law. Those are company claims, rather than independently established results. The public launch follows restricted testing by selected cyber defenders, and access initially goes to higher-tier paying subscribers. Introductory pricing is two dollars per million input tokens and ten dollars per million output tokens, around a fifth of its leading rivals’ prices. Rates double at the end of November, but remain substantially lower per token; actual task costs will depend on how many tokens each model uses. Google is back in frontier competition with a sharp price challenge.

An OpenAI employee has been arrested and charged with covertly intercepting conversations after a recording device was found in an executive conference room. Federal prosecutors say Abdulrahman Alfozan placed the device behind a television in the company’s San Francisco office; OpenAI fired him, and he was arrested on October ninth. TechCrunch reports that the company describes encrypted audio monitoring and a data-transfer mechanism designed to evade detection. The purpose remains unclear. OpenAI says it has no indication that model weights, source code or customer data were improperly accessed or extracted.

Ukraine says it received no advance warning from partners before Russian ballistic missiles struck Kyiv on successive nights. The Guardian reports that Tuesday’s attacks hit a critical power plant and an apartment building, killing one person and injuring thirteen. The missing warnings follow American threats to halt intelligence sharing, but President Trump says sharing continues, and Ukraine’s foreign minister attributed the warning gap to a special type of Russian weaponry. There is no confirmed intelligence cutoff. Ukrainian commanders say that intelligence is crucial for warning of missile launches, as Russia continues attacking the power grid despite Trump’s claimed energy ceasefire.

Saudi Arabia and the Houthis are escalating attacks toward energy infrastructure. The Guardian reports that Saudi forces struck oil depots near Sana’a and at Ras Isa, while the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted a missile aimed at Dharan, where Saudi Aramco has its headquarters. The Houthis have threatened Aramco facilities, extending the confrontation beyond the airport attacks already disrupting Riyadh. On Polymarket, the probability of Houthi military action against Saudi Arabia on October fifteenth has risen from forty-eight percent a week ago to sixty-five percent.
