Here is your daily news briefing for Saturday, March twenty-ninth.

A federal judge has formally rebuked the administration’s attempt to blacklist the AI firm Anthropic, stating that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and President Trump exceeded their legal authority. The ruling clarifies that the Department of Defense failed to provide a valid national security justification for designating the lab as a "supply chain risk." This decision reinforces the legal shield for AI developers against arbitrary government exclusion, even as the administration maintains its "silicon sovereignty" push.

The cybersecurity landscape has been shaken by a confirmed breach of F-B-I Director Kash Patel’s personal email account. A hacking group linked to the Iranian government, known as Handala, has published documents and photos purportedly taken from Patel's Gmail account in what they claim is retaliation for his pledge to "hunt" foreign hackers. While the F-B-I maintains the leaked information is "historical in nature," the breach of the nation’s top domestic security official highlights the extreme vulnerability of high-value targets to state-sponsored cyber operations.

In the Middle East, Senator Marco Rubio states the United States expects to "finish" the war with Iran within the next couple of weeks. This aggressive timeline comes as U.S. allies begin preparations to escort tankers through the Strait of Hormuz, which remains a volatile chokepoint. However, the human cost of the conflict is drawing intense international scrutiny; the United Nations has called for a formal probe into a recent strike on an Iranian school that killed at least one hundred and sixty-eight people, mostly children.

The hardware sector is facing a severe supply crunch known as "RAM-mageddon," which is now driving up prices for consumers. Sony has announced it will raise the price of the PlayStation five by as much as one hundred and fifty dollars, citing critical shortages in memory and storage components. To combat this, South Korean memory giant S-K Hynix is reportedly planning a fourteen-billion-dollar initial public offering in the United States to fund massive capacity expansion. Simultaneously, Apple has officially discontinued the Mac Pro desktop, signaling an end to its highest-end workstation as it pivots toward more efficient, integrated silicon.

OpenAI is moving to evolve its business model beyond simple chat interfaces. Following the recent shutdown of its Sora video project, the company has launched a new "Plugins" feature for its Codex model. This allows its AI to move beyond writing code to executing tasks across third-party software environments, directly competing with the "Computer Use" capabilities recently showcased by Anthropic. As OpenAI refocuses on productivity, rumors are intensifying that its backer, SoftBank, is securing a forty-billion-dollar loan to facilitate an OpenAI initial public offering as early as next year.

Looking toward tomorrow, keep an eye on the United States House of Representatives, where a high-stakes vote on Department of Homeland Security funding is scheduled in an attempt to end the government shutdown and relieve the mounting chaos at national airports.
