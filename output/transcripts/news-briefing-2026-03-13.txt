Here is your daily news briefing for Friday, March thirteenth.

The geopolitical crisis in the Middle East has entered a period of entrenched hostility following the formal succession of Iran's leadership. In his first public statement since the death of his father, the new Supreme Leader, Mojtaba Khamenei, vowed to fully block the Strait of Hormuz and continue strikes against United States bases in the region. This hardline stance coincides with a report from the United Arab Emirates claiming that over one thousand eight hundred drones and missiles have been projected at the country since the conflict began. Meanwhile, in Washington, the Department of Defense is facing mounting questions from lawmakers regarding whether United States munitions were responsible for the strike on a school in Tehran earlier this week, an incident Secretary Pete Hegseth says is currently under investigation.

In the escalating conflict between the federal government and the AI industry, the legal battle over Anthropic’s "supply chain risk" designation is intensifying. Internal documents suggest the Pentagon's blacklisting may be tied to Anthropic's refusal to integrate its models into autonomous lethal targeting systems. This friction arrives as the industry's focus shifts toward "agentic" AI—models that can take actions on a user's behalf. Google and Samsung have begun rolling out "Gemini task automation," allowing the AI to independently handle food delivery and rideshare bookings. Not to be outdone, Perplexity has launched a "Personal Computer" tool that turns a spare Mac into a twenty-four-seven automated proxy that can manage files and execute tasks locally.

In the realm of multimodal capabilities, Anthropic has updated its Claude models to natively generate charts, diagrams, and flowcharts directly within the chat interface. Rather than providing static images, the system inserts live visualizations to explain complex data trends or architectural designs. Microsoft is making a similar push into specialized reasoning with "Copilot Health," a secure environment that allows users to connect their medical records and wearable data to the AI for clinical analysis and lab result interpretation.

The business of AI-driven labor is seeing a wave of aggressive valuations and corporate restructuring. The human-centered workforce is feeling the pressure; Atlassian announced today it is laying off ten percent of its staff—roughly one thousand six hundred people—to reallocate capital toward AI development. Despite these cuts, the venture market remains overheated. Sales automation startup Rox AI reached a one billion two hundred million dollar valuation today, while Sunday, a humanoid robotics firm building household assistants, hit a one billion one hundred and fifty million dollar valuation with over one thousand customers already on a waitlist for its "Memo" robot.

Finally, in hardware, the electric vehicle market is seeing a massive retreat from the American manufacturing sector. Honda has officially canceled three of its United States-made EV models, citing high losses and shifting emissions regulations. Conversely, Tesla is expanding its footprint by becoming a certified electricity utility in the United Kingdom, positioning itself to sell power directly to consumers and set up a direct showdown with traditional European energy providers.

As we look toward Monday, the tech world will be focused on Nvidia’s flagship conference where CEO Jensen Huang is expected to debut the next generation of silicon designed specifically to power the "agentic" workflows currently being deployed by Google and Perplexity.
