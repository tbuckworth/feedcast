Here is your daily news briefing for Saturday, November twenty-first, twenty twenty-six.

An Anthropic mathematician, Levent Alpöge, says he has solved the Riemann hypothesis, the problem about the hidden structure of prime numbers that dates back to eighteen fifty-nine and is one of the Millennium Prize problems. He says he did it with Claude. TechCrunch reports that the proof is written in Lean, a language that lets a computer formally check a proof. Several mathematicians, including Andrew Sutherland of MIT, say they have confirmed that it compiles. The catch is that no one yet has an explanation a human can follow. Alpöge admits he feels some guilt about releasing it that way and has committed to producing one. He also messaged Dario Amodei last Sunday to ask how to announce it, and called the timing, in the week of the listing, "horrible." Alpöge and New York University's Tristan Buckmaster also released a proof related to the Navier-Stokes equations. On the same day, Stanford's Ravi Vakil posted a draft proof of the general Riemann hypothesis by postdoc Alessandro Masullo, which he stressed is "all human, with no AI." Without a Lean version, it is too early to say whether that proof holds. Polymarket gives ten percent that the Clay Institute declares the hypothesis solved by the end of next year, barely above a week ago. That market resolves yes if the Clay Mathematics Institute awards a Millennium Prize for the Riemann Hypothesis, or otherwise officially declares it solved on its website or in an official communication, by eleven fifty-nine p.m. Eastern Time on December thirty-first, twenty twenty-seven.

On safety, OpenAI has described how it fights adversarial distillation, where someone trains a model on a frontier model's outputs and gets its capabilities without its safeguards. Its main target is the theft of chain-of-thought reasoning. In OpenAI's internal study, students trained on exact reasoning traces generally picked up more cyber capability than those trained on summaries, even when the original tasks were defensive. In OpenAI's own red-teaming, the success rate for extracting reasoning fell from fifty-four percent against GPT-6 Astra to zero point two percent against GPT-6.2 Astra.

Signal, meanwhile, is blocking screenshots by default on iOS and Android to stop operating-system agents like Siri and Gemini from reading chats. Its president, Meredith Whittaker, calls agent integration in operating systems "an existential threat to privacy at the application layer." She admits the fix is incomplete, because agents can still reach data through the file system. Signal says it cannot offer the protection on macOS at all, and it is pressing Apple, Google and Microsoft for proper tools.
