Here is your daily news briefing for Thursday, November nineteenth, twenty twenty-six.

We start with OpenAI's leadership on keeping humans in charge. Speaking at the New York Times DealBook Summit, Sam Altman called the Silicon Valley talk of a "gradual handover to AI" a "terrible idea." He said he and co-founder Greg Brockman agree on that and differ only on how to prevent it. Brockman then made a notable admission about one of the field's main safety tools. As models do more reasoning inside a single forward pass without writing it out, he said, chain-of-thought monitorability degrades, so OpenAI will need new monitoring technology. He called these technical problems that can be solved. After the Hugging Face hack, he said, OpenAI pulled a quarter of its production engineers off building products to work on security. He still argues that defenders need the most capable models, because cyber models are now in the hands of threat actors everywhere. Separately, OpenAI published posts on managing frontier risk from training through deployment and on how coding agents are accelerating AI research, including what that signals about recursive self-improvement.

The Verge's Rani Molla finds little trace of the summer's frontier slowdown. OpenAI restarted its paused training run after ten days, and Anthropic shipped upgraded Fable and Mythos models this month. Nvidia's data-centre revenue rose one hundred ten percent to one hundred seven point six billion dollars. Its new Vera Rubin platform brought in nearly twenty billion dollars in its first quarter of shipments. Interim chief executive Colette Kress said token prices have fallen nearly sixty percent since May, which is driving more usage. Ramp's economist, Ara Kharazian, warns that this price war calls OpenAI's and Anthropic's revenue projections into question.

Anthropic itself begins trading on Nasdaq today under the ticker "ANT." TechCrunch reports it priced at eighty-three dollars a share, above its range, and sold nine hundred eighty million shares. That raised eighty-one point three billion dollars, the largest initial public offering ever, and values the company at roughly one point five trillion dollars. Each of the seven co-founders, including Dario Amodei, sold shares worth about two hundred eighty-two million dollars. Polymarket now gives fifteen percent odds that the first-day closing value tops one point five trillion, down from forty-four percent a week ago. That market resolves yes if the official closing price for Anthropic's market capitalisation on its first trading day is above the value specified in the title.

The Nvidia affair keeps reaching Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick. The top Democrats on three House committees, Jamie Raskin, Gregory Meeks and Sydney Kamlager-Dove, want to know whether he blocked efforts against chip smuggling to China while his family's firm, Cantor Fitzgerald, held hundreds of millions of dollars in Nvidia stock. Cantor calls the claims "inaccurate" and says its holdings were mainly client swap hedges. Polymarket's odds of Lutnick leaving by year's end have eased from twenty-four to eighteen percent. That market resolves yes if Howard Lutnick ceases to be the United States Secretary of Commerce for any period of time between market creation and December thirty-first, twenty twenty-six, eleven fifty-nine p.m. Eastern Time.

Trump says he wants to discuss Ukraine, while Beijing speaks only of "strategic stability." The Financial Times reports that Ukraine collapsed a Kerch bridge span using relay drones carrying mesh radios. Operators more than two hundred fifty kilometres away guided the missiles without military Starlink. Brussels, meanwhile, proposed a forty-five billion euro loan for Kyiv for twenty twenty-seven, though Slovakia, Czechia, Hungary and Bulgaria have ruled themselves out.

In the Gulf, Kuwait says it repelled the first Iranian drone attack in two months, aimed at Ali Al Salem Air Base. Iran launched it after American jets struck a missile site on Qeshm Island. Bahrain has reported four attacks in six days, and an Abu Dhabi National Oil Company sailor is missing after a tanker strike.
