Here is your daily news briefing for Thursday, May twenty-second.

The long-awaited initial public offering of SpaceX has officially moved into the public eye, revealing the first comprehensive look at Elon Musk’s integrated business empire. The filing confirms that SpaceX is the financial linchpin for Musk’s artificial intelligence ambitions, revealing that xAI lost six point four billion dollars last year and is committing two point eight billion dollars to natural gas turbines to power its data centers. Most notably, the documents disclose a surprising multi-billion dollar alliance between rivals: Anthropic is paying xAI one billion two hundred fifty million dollars per month for compute power. Market analysts suggest the offering could make Musk the world’s first trillionaire, though the filing explicitly lists Musk’s complex cross-company financial "shuffling" as a primary risk factor for investors.

In the AI capability race, OpenAI appears to have achieved a milestone in machine reasoning. The company claims its latest models have disproved a geometry conjecture that has remained unsolved since nineteen forty-six. Unlike previous claims that were dismissed by the academic community, prominent mathematicians have reportedly verified this specific proof. This breakthrough coincided with reports that OpenAI is accelerating its own initial public offering, potentially as early as September, following its recent legal victory over Elon Musk. To further solidify its ecosystem, Chief Executive Officer Sam Altman has made a "mic drop" offer to the current Y Combinator class, proposing to invest OpenAI tokens directly into every startup in exchange for equity.

Google is moving to monetize its new "agentic" search interface by integrating Al-generated advertising directly into Gemini conversational flows. When users interact with the new search box, the AI will now generate "custom explainers" justifying why a user should purchase specific sponsored products. This expansion of the AI-driven business model comes as industry experts warn of the physical toll of the AI boom. In Utah, public backlash has intensified over the "Stratos Project," a proposed forty thousand acre data center in Box Elder County. Despite warnings regarding extreme water and power consumption, local officials have signed off on the project, framing it as a necessity for American AI dominance.

Geopolitically, the "no-limits" partnership between Russia and China is showing its first signs of visible friction. Following a high-profile summit in Beijing, President Vladimir Putin departed without a finalized deal for the Power of Siberia Two gas pipeline. While President Xi Jinping maintained a display of solidarity, the lack of a pipeline agreement suggests Beijing is leveraging its superior bargaining position as Russia’s primary economic lifeline. Simultaneously, the United States has escalated legal pressure on the Caribbean, charging former Cuban leader Raúl Castro with murder in connection to the nineteen ninety-six downing of two American civilian planes. This move, which Havana calls a "political maneuver," marks a significant hardening of the Trump administration’s stance toward the island.

The global health crisis continues to worsen in the Democratic Republic of Congo. The World Health Organization now reports one hundred thirty-nine suspected deaths and six hundred cases in the ongoing Ebola outbreak. Officials warned today that a viable vaccine roll-out could take up to nine months due to the rare strain involved and the difficulty of operating in an active conflict zone. This delay increases the risk of prolonged disruption to the regional mining operations that provide the cobalt essential for the global lithium-ion battery supply chain.

Looking toward tomorrow, tech regulators will be monitoring the first day of a landmark trial regarding the "right to tweak" smart TV software, a case that could determine whether consumers have the legal right to strip advertising and tracking code from their own devices.
