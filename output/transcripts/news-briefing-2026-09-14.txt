Here is your daily news briefing for Monday, September fourteenth, two-thousand twenty-six.

The dominant story today is the political backlash against the AI industry's call for a slowdown. Following Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's proposal over the weekend to "pace the frontier," and public endorsements from OpenAI's Sam Altman and Elon Musk, President Trump and House Speaker Mike Johnson pushed back hard. According to the Financial Times, Trump said "whoever wins AI, wins" and dismissed safety warnings, saying people are "bringing up things that won't happen." Johnson told CNN that if Congress rushes to regulate AI, "we will lose the race to China, and that is a threat to every single American." This sets up a sharp political divide. Democratic House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries called for "decisive action" to slow the pace of development, and former President Obama reportedly told a Democratic fundraiser that AI should be a "central agenda" for the party, saying the technology is "moving very fast in private hands" and could be dangerous without oversight. The New York Times reports Obama has offered himself as a "sounding board" to AI executives and has spoken with both Amodei and Altman. Trump's AI adviser David Sacks was more pointed, telling Amodei that the easiest way not to build superintelligence is to simply agree not to build it, and that demanding a preferred regulatory framework as the price of restraint "will look like blackmail of the public and the political system." This framing — that the labs should just stop on their own rather than seeking government involvement — could become the administration's primary line of defense against regulation.

Meanwhile, the AI safety debate itself is drawing skepticism from parts of Silicon Valley. At the Goldman Sachs conference in San Francisco, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang reportedly dismissed the warnings from former Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon as untrue. Grindr's CEO called Anthropic's public statements "dangerous" and said he had instructed engineers to stop using its technology. The Guardian quotes Professor Stuart Russell, one of the world's leading AI experts, criticizing Amodei's approach as "completely backwards," arguing that safety requirements should be met before further progress occurs, not that development should merely slow to buy time. David Krueger, an AI professor and former founding director of the UK's AI Security Institute, went further, calling for "an immediate, indefinite, international moratorium on frontier AI development." The BBC reports broad uncertainty about what a slowdown would actually mean in practice, with multiple observers noting that no one has offered a concrete operational definition.

In geopolitics, the BRICS summit in Delhi concluded with a joint declaration that carefully avoids assigning blame on any major conflict. The statement calls for "maximum restraint" in the Middle East and condemns attacks on civilian infrastructure and nuclear facilities but names no countries. Notably, it makes no mention of Russia's war in Ukraine — a departure from previous BRICS declarations. The summit did produce a significant diplomatic moment: Iran's president and Abu Dhabi's Crown Prince held what is reported to be the highest-level face-to-face meeting between the two countries since the Middle East conflict began. Chinese President Xi Jinping visited Delhi for the first time in seven years for brief talks with Indian Prime Minister Modi, though Xi stayed less than twenty-four hours and skipped the gala dinner. On the ground in Yemen, the Houthis claimed an attack on a Saudi military base as fighting with Yemeni forces continued to flare.

Separately, a Russian drone struck a train near the Poland-Ukraine border in what Ukrainian Railways said may have been an attempt to target a diplomatic train carrying former British Prime Minister Boris Johnson and former CIA chief David Petraeus, which had departed the station shortly before the strike. The attack was part of a wave of more than four hundred fifty drones and missiles launched at Ukraine overnight, killing at least six people. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk convened an emergency meeting, warning that Russian escalation in the coming weeks could affect Polish territory.

Looking ahead, the Guardian notes that Donald Trump's meeting with Xi Jinping in Washington on September twenty-fourth will test the prospects of any Sino-American coordination on AI safety — a key element of Amodei's third step for global regulation.
