Here is your daily news briefing for February eighteenth, twenty twenty-six.

We begin with a major development in AI infrastructure. Meta has struck a multiyear deal with Nvidia to expand its data centers with millions of Grace and Vera CPUs alongside Blackwell and Rubin GPUs. While Meta has long used Nvidia hardware for AI products, this represents the first large-scale Nvidia Grace-only deployment, which Nvidia says will deliver significant performance-per-watt improvements. The scale of this commitment underscores the infrastructure arms race among major AI companies as they compete to secure the compute capacity needed for next-generation models.

This comes as the AI industry grapples with a growing memory crisis. According to TechCrunch, when we discuss AI infrastructure costs, the focus typically falls on Nvidia and GPUs, but memory is becoming an increasingly critical bottleneck. The RAM shortage has reached the point where it's affecting even established products. Valve reports that its four-year-old Steam Deck is now only available intermittently due to memory supply constraints. Framework, the do-it-yourself PC maker, says it needs monthly price increases to navigate the acute shortage and expects conditions to worsen before improving. Running AI models is turning into what TechCrunch characterizes as "a memory game," with memory access patterns and bandwidth becoming as important as raw compute power.

In AI model developments, Anthropic has released Sonnet four point six, keeping pace with its four-month update cycle. This mid-sized model continues Anthropic's strategy of regular incremental improvements rather than splashy launches. Meanwhile, Mistral AI has made its first acquisition, buying Paris-based Koyeb to back its cloud ambitions. Koyeb simplifies AI app deployment at scale and manages the infrastructure behind it, suggesting Mistral is building out a complete stack from models to deployment infrastructure.

OpenAI faces legal challenges on multiple fronts. A U.S. court has barred the company from using the name "Cameo" for a feature in Sora, forcing OpenAI to rename it to "Characters." This trademark dispute highlights the legal complications that arise when AI companies choose names that overlap with existing brands. The renaming may seem minor, but it reflects the broader challenge of navigating intellectual property law while rapidly deploying new features.

The AI wearables space is heating up significantly. Bloomberg reports that Apple is planning to launch AI-powered glasses, a pendant, and camera-equipped AirPods. All three devices will include built-in cameras and connect to the iPhone, allowing Siri to use visual context to carry out actions. This represents Apple's entry into the ambient AI assistant category, where devices continuously observe the environment to provide contextual assistance. The move puts Apple in direct competition with Meta's Ray-Ban smart glasses and other emerging AI wearable products.

In the enterprise AI sector, WordPress has added an AI Assistant that can edit content, adjust styles, create images, and make changes using natural language commands. The feature is designed to work inside websites to understand their content and layout without requiring precisely tailored prompts. This integration of AI directly into content management systems reflects the ongoing embedding of AI capabilities into core productivity tools.

Google has announced it will display links more prominently inside AI-powered features in Search. A list of links will now appear in a pop-up when users hover over sources in AI Overviews and AI Mode on desktop, alongside descriptions of each source. This change responds to concerns that AI-generated summaries reduce traffic to original sources by making the underlying links less visible. The company has also announced dates for I/O twenty twenty-six, which will take place May nineteenth to twentieth in Mountain View, California, where Google says it will share the latest AI breakthroughs and updates across products from Gemini to Android.

The European Union has launched what it describes as a "large-scale" investigation into xAI over sexualized images. The probe could result in massive fines if xAI is found to have violated EU regulations. Separately, Shein is under EU investigation over childlike sex dolls, with Europe examining whether the fast fashion giant breached the Digital Services Act. These enforcement actions signal that European regulators are moving aggressively to use existing laws to constrain AI systems that generate problematic content.

The European Parliament has taken a more direct approach, blocking AI tools on lawmakers' devices entirely, citing security risks. EU lawmakers found their government-issued devices blocked from using baked-in AI tools amid fears that sensitive information could turn up on U.S. servers of AI companies. This represents a significant institutional barrier to AI adoption, driven by sovereignty and security concerns rather than capability questions.

Several countries are moving to ban social media for children, with TechCrunch reporting that Australia was the first to issue such a ban in late twenty twenty-five, aiming to reduce pressures and risks young users face including cyberbullying, social media addiction, and exposure to predators. Other nations are now following this precedent, reflecting growing concern about social media's impact on youth mental health.

Meta's own internal research has found that parental supervision may not help teens regulate their social media use, and that teens with trauma are more inclined to overuse social media. This internal finding undercuts one of the platform's key defenses against criticism of its effects on young users. The revelation that Meta's own research shows parental controls are ineffective raises questions about whether the company has been promoting solutions it knows don't work.

In venture capital, several significant funding rounds closed. Thrive Capital raised ten billion dollars for its new fund, nearly double the size of its last fund, making it one of the largest venture raises in recent memory. Jack Altman has joined Benchmark as a general partner, bringing his operational experience to one of Silicon Valley's most established firms. Climactic has launched a hybrid fund called Material Scale to help climate tech startups, particularly in the apparel industry, navigate what it calls the "valley of death" between early-stage funding and commercial scale.

TechCrunch reports that seventeen U.S.-based AI companies have raised one hundred million dollars or more so far in twenty twenty-six, with three raising rounds larger than one billion dollars. This capital influx continues despite broader concerns about when these investments will generate returns. SpaceX veterans raised fifty million dollars in Series A funding for Mesh, which aims to mass-produce optical transceivers for AI data centers, addressing another infrastructure bottleneck beyond GPUs and memory.

India is making a major push to attract AI infrastructure investment. The country is ramping up shared AI compute, adding twenty thousand GPUs as part of a broader effort to attract over two hundred billion dollars in AI investment by twenty twenty-eight. This represents India's bid to become a major AI infrastructure hub rather than remaining dependent on capacity in other countries.

In media and entertainment, Warner Bros. has rejected Paramount's acquisition offer again but asked for a "best and final offer." The WB board officially recommends a Netflix deal but is giving Paramount one more chance to increase its price. This reflects ongoing consolidation pressures in the streaming industry as companies seek scale.

Apple has announced a "Special Experience" on March fourth, with expectations running high for new Macs and iPads. Apple has not confirmed whether the event will be available to watch live, though coverage will be available from multiple outlets. Separately, Apple Podcasts is getting an enhanced video experience this spring, bringing improved video podcast viewing to its app in an apparent effort to keep users from turning to rival platforms like YouTube and Spotify.

Amazon's Fire TV is rolling out a new interface in the U.S. that simplifies layout and navigation while adding enhanced Alexa integration. The update reflects the company's ongoing effort to make its streaming platform more competitive as the market consolidates.

In automotive technology, Ford is turning to Formula One thinking and bounty programs to build a thirty-thousand-dollar electric truck by twenty twenty-seven. The team, led by Tesla veteran Alan Clarke, is using a combination of three-D-printed Lego-like parts and an obsession with efficiency to hit that target price point. A smaller battery means a cheaper truck, though customers still expect plenty of range, creating a significant engineering challenge. Ford believes efficiency improvements across the entire vehicle can make up for reduced battery capacity. The Toyota bZ Woodland, meanwhile, offers what Ars Technica describes as looking "a lot like an electric station wagon" with added ground clearance and cargo room making the bZ more compelling in a crowded EV market.

Tesla has dodged a thirty-day suspension in California after removing Autopilot, its basic driver-assistance system. The decision comes a month after Tesla discontinued Autopilot amid regulatory pressure. The company's retreat from the Autopilot branding, which critics argued was misleading, suggests regulatory scrutiny is having its intended effect.

In cybersecurity developments, Amnesty International says it found evidence that a government customer of Intellexa, a sanctioned surveillance vendor, used its Predator spyware to hack the iPhone of a prominent journalist in Angola. This represents another instance of commercial spyware being used to target journalists and activists, highlighting the ongoing challenge of controlling proliferation of these tools even after sanctions are imposed.

VMware users continue fleeing Broadcom's platform, with a new CloudBolt survey finding that most are "actively reducing their VMware footprint." The report notes that Broadcom's strategy was never to keep every customer, but the scale of customer departures may exceed even Broadcom's expectations. The migration away from VMware is creating opportunities for alternative virtualization platforms but causing significant disruption for enterprises that built their infrastructure around VMware products.

The sustainability of connected car features is raising new questions. Ars Technica asks what happens to a car when the company behind its software goes under, noting that connected car servers won't be online indefinitely and startups often go bust. This creates a long-term support problem for vehicles that increasingly depend on cloud services for core features. Unlike traditional cars where all functionality is self-contained, connected vehicles can lose features if backend services shut down.

In password security, contrary to what password managers claim, a server compromise can mean game over. Ars Technica reports that password managers' promise that they can't see users' vaults isn't always true, challenging a core security claim these services make. The revelation highlights that even well-designed security systems can have vulnerabilities in their server-side implementation.

Stephen Colbert says CBS forbid an interview with a Texas Democrat because of FCC threats, characterizing the decision as "purely financial" rather than editorial. The network denies it stopped the interview from airing but warned it could run afoul of the U.S. broadcast media watchdog. This incident reflects growing concerns about government pressure on media companies affecting editorial decisions.

In international developments, the first day of Russia-Ukraine peace talks has ended in Geneva. U.S. special envoy Steve Witkoff said "meaningful progress" was made, striking an optimistic note, though expectations for a breakthrough remain low. Negotiations continue today, but fundamental gaps between the parties' positions suggest any agreement will be difficult to reach.

Iran says "guiding principles" were agreed with the U.S. at nuclear talks, though Tehran acknowledges more work is needed to get a deal. The U.S. has not yet commented on the issue. These talks represent a potential diplomatic opening, though significant distrust remains on both sides.

The investigation into Stanfield Airport private flights over Epstein ties continues, with Essex Police assessing information about flights into and out of the airport. This is part of the broader ongoing examination of networks connected to Jeffrey Epstein's operation.

Civil rights leader Jesse Jackson has died at age eighty-four. Tributes poured in from politicians who described Jackson as an agent of change and transformative leader. Jackson's influence spread from the churches of the Deep South to the White House, and his life as a Martin Luther King protege helped reshape America. In nineteen ninety-nine, Jackson flew to Yugoslavia to help free three U.S. soldiers captured during the Kosovo War, demonstrating his continued activism and diplomatic efforts even decades after the civil rights movement's peak years. One of those freed soldiers recalled that "He did it for us."

Peru's president has been impeached just four months into his term. José Jerí becomes the third consecutive president to be ousted and the country's seventh since twenty sixteen. This represents continued political instability in Peru as the country cycles through leaders unable to complete their terms.

The Philippines' Vice President Sara Duterte has announced a presidential run in twenty twenty-eight. The daughter of former President Rodrigo Duterte has feuded spectacularly with incumbent Bongbong Marcos, setting up what could be a contentious election.

In the ongoing Nancy Guthrie abduction case, police say there are no DNA matches on the glove found near her home. The sheriff told the BBC he is confident they will find her, whether it takes ten days, ten months, or worse. The case continues to generate intense public interest given Savannah Guthrie's prominence.

Separately, a dark web investigation has led to the rescue of a twelve-year-old girl from years of harm. Detectives desperate to locate the child, seen being abused online, found a surprising lead through a bedroom wall clue that allowed them to identify and rescue the victim. The case demonstrates the painstaking work involved in tracking down victims of online exploitation.

A climber is on trial for leaving his girlfriend to die on Austria's highest mountain. Kerstin G's boyfriend is accused of leaving her unprotected and exhausted close to the summit during a blizzard, raising questions about duty of care in extreme mountaineering situations.

A suspect in a Rhode Island ice hockey shooting has been found dead inside the Pawtucket arena. Robert Dorgan, who also went by the name Roberta Esposito, had killed his ex-wife and son before the shooting, according to police.

Under pressure from Trump, Venezuela's new president has what BBC describes as "aces up her sleeve." Delcy Rodriguez knows it's in America's interests for her to be a success, giving her leverage in negotiations with the U.S. administration.

In a bizarre historical discovery, scientists hunting mammoth fossils found whales four hundred kilometers inland, raising more questions than it answers about ancient geography and climate. Sometimes new data creates mysteries rather than solving them.

Looking ahead, the convergence of infrastructure constraints, particularly around memory and power, suggests AI scaling may face more fundamental bottlenecks than previously anticipated. The regulatory divergence between regions, with the EU taking aggressive enforcement action while the U.S. remains more permissive, is creating a fragmented global landscape for AI deployment. And the continued political instability across multiple countries, from Peru to the Philippines, combined with the ongoing Russia-Ukraine conflict and Iran nuclear talks, reflects a broader unraveling of established political orders that will shape how AI technology is developed and deployed globally.
