Here is your daily news briefing for Saturday, July fifth, two thousand twenty-six.

In a significant diplomatic development, Chinese authorities have released Jin Mingri, the founder of the Zion Church, following a direct personal plea from President Donald Trump to Xi Jinping. Jin, a prominent figure in China’s underground church movement, had been imprisoned during a multi-year crackdown on unregistered religious groups. This release suggests a tactical opening in U.S.-China relations, where high-level personal intervention is successfully securing humanitarian concessions despite broader trade and technological friction between the two superpowers.

The global landscape of A-I security and corporate competition is tightening as Alibaba has reportedly banned its employees from using Anthropic’s new "Claude Code" tool. The Chinese tech giant officially classified the software as "high-risk," citing concerns over sensitive data exfiltration and intellectual property security. This move highlights a growing trend of "software sovereignty," where major international firms are increasingly blocking foreign agentic A-I tools to prevent their proprietary codebases from being used to train competing frontier models.

In legal and intellectual property news, Midjourney is escalating its defense against Hollywood. As part of an ongoing lawsuit, the generative A-I startup is seeking to compel major studios to disclose their own internal use of artificial intelligence. Midjourney’s legal team argues that if studios are leveraging A-I for production while simultaneously suing labs for copyright infringement, it exposes a double standard regarding "fair use." This discovery process could force Hollywood to reveal the extent to which synthetic media has already permeated the film industry’s professional workflows.

Geopolitical stability in the Middle East faces a critical test today as the state funeral for Ayatollah Ali Khamenei begins in Tehran. Iranian authorities expect up to twenty million people to participate in ceremonies across Iran and Iraq over the coming days. Observers describe this as an "intensely political moment" that will determine the successor to the Supreme Leader. The transition comes at a time of extreme regional tension, and the scale of the mourning serves as a massive, government-orchestrated display of strength amidst internal and external pressures.

Finally, in South America, Keiko Fujimori has been officially declared the winner of Peru’s presidential election, nearly a month after the initial vote. The daughter of former president Alberto Fujimori secured victory in an extremely narrow race that was stalled by weeks of legal challenges and fraud allegations. Her presidency is expected to bring a return to conservative economic policies, though she faces a deeply polarized electorate and a legislature that remains skeptical of the Fujimori legacy.

Expect global energy markets to react as Ukraine continues its campaign against Russian infrastructure, following a successful strike on a major oil terminal in Saint Petersburg.
