Here is your daily news briefing for Thursday, September tenth, two-thousand twenty-six.

The lead story today is a dramatic escalation of concern inside the AI industry about existential risk. Jacob Coxon, a researcher who trained AI systems at both OpenAI and Anthropic, publicly resigned from Anthropic, accusing both companies of "racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives." What makes this extraordinary is what happened next. Evan Hubinger, who leads one of Anthropic's own safety teams, responded publicly to agree, saying — and I quote — "We really do earnestly believe AI could kill all humans," and personally estimated the probability at greater than one in ten within the next decade. Hubinger added that Anthropic does "not yet have a plan" for ensuring advanced AI remains safe and is "not clearly on track to" develop one. This triggered a swift political response. Republican Senator Ted Cruz called it a "catastrophic risk." Independent Senator Bernie Sanders cited polling showing Americans overwhelmingly want to ban artificial superintelligence and pause AI development until clear safety standards exist. Democrat Representative Ted Lieu called for immediate passage of the bipartisan AI Kill Switch Bill, and Representative Lori Trahan said it was "past time for Congress to get off the sidelines." This is significant because these warnings are not coming from outside critics — they are coming from the people building the systems, and the political response is now bipartisan and urgent.

On a related front, OpenAI made two notable governance moves. First, the company appointed Paul Christiano, a prominent figure in AI alignment and safety research, to its Foundation Board and its Safety and Security Committee. Second, OpenAI's chief policy officer Chris Lehane published a call for stronger safety evidence, shared standards, and durable policy action while what he called "the policy window" remains open. These moves come as OpenAI continues managing fallout from the Navier-Stokes controversy, which The Verge reports has sent a chill through the mathematics community. Mathematicians describe OpenAI's apparent rush to beat independent researchers to a result as a violation of deeply embedded norms of trust and openness that the discipline depends on.

Turning to national security, US intelligence agencies — the NSA, CISA, and FBI — jointly accused six Chinese AI firms, including DeepSeek, Alibaba, Moonshot AI, MiniMax, StepFun, and Z.AI, of conducting what they called industrial-scale distillation attacks against American frontier AI models since at least late two-thousand twenty-four. The agencies said these firms likely acted with Chinese government awareness, extracting capabilities from variants of Claude, GPT, Gemini, and Grok through methods including bulk purchases of fake accounts to run coordinated queries and prompt injection techniques to force models to reveal their hidden chain-of-thought reasoning. The agencies called for coordinated action across the entire AI ecosystem to combat what they described as aggressive, malicious theft threatening the US lead in AI.

On the global economy, the US Treasury's attempt to calm bond markets by buying back six billion dollars in government debt was swiftly rebuffed. Yields on ten-year Treasuries rose to a three-year high, and the thirty-year yield hit approximately five-point-two percent — the highest since the two-thousand-eight financial crisis. US government debt now exceeds forty trillion dollars. Meanwhile, oil prices climbed back above one hundred dollars a barrel as the Iran conflict continued to escalate. Brent crude is up more than sixty percent this year. The UN nuclear watchdog referred Iran to the Security Council for non-compliance with the non-proliferation treaty for the first time in twenty years, citing lack of access to nuclear facilities and insufficient information about Iran's highly enriched uranium stockpile. Iran blamed US and Israeli strikes on its nuclear sites for disrupting inspections. New sanctions are unlikely given China and Russia hold vetoes.

And in German politics, Chancellor Merz escalated his confrontation with the far-right AfD, accusing the party of pursuing "ethnic cleansing" through its remigration agenda. He also cancelled a planned phone call with President Trump after Trump celebrated the AfD's election victory on social media. The AfD is now polling in first place nationally at twenty-eight to twenty-nine percent ahead of two more state elections later this month.

Looking ahead, the extraordinary public admissions from inside Anthropic about existential risk, combined with growing bipartisan momentum in Congress, suggest the political debate over AI safety regulation may be entering an entirely new phase.
